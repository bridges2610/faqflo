Roofing sits at an unusual intersection. The jobs are large, the decisions are urgent, and the
customer usually knows nothing about the product they’re buying. That combination makes them ask
for advice — and asking has quietly moved from “call my brother-in-law” to “ask ChatGPT.”

Three roofing situations, three different AI answers, all worth winning:

**The leak.** Water is coming through a ceiling. They need a name in the next ten minutes. Nobody’s
comparing shingle warranties.

**The storm.** A hail or wind event goes through, and half the neighborhood is suddenly asking the
same questions at the same time — is this claimable, who does insurance work, is that door-knocker
legitimate. Every one of those is a question an AI will answer, and the answer names companies.

**The replacement.** A roof at end of life, researched over weeks. Costs, materials, financing,
warranties, whether to go metal. The AI is functioning as a shortlist builder here, and the
shortlist is short.

Getting named in those answers is what people mean by AEO — Answer Engine Optimization. Here’s the
roofing version.

## First: can AI read your site at all?

Before anything clever, check this. It’s the most common failure and it’s invisible from the
outside.

**Your content might be rendered by JavaScript.** If your services, financing details, or FAQ
appear after the page loads — which is how nearly every FAQ plugin and accordion widget works — AI
crawlers generally don’t see it. They fetch the raw HTML and move on without running scripts.

**Crawlers might be blocked.** Check `yoursite.com/robots.txt` for `Disallow: /` under `GPTBot`,
`OAI-SearchBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, or `Google-Extended`. This is almost always
accidental — inherited from a theme or an old developer rule.

**Your details might be trapped in images.** Service area in a map graphic, financing terms in a
PDF, manufacturer certifications in a logo strip. A person reads all of that. A crawler reads none
of it.

Twenty-second check: open your live site, right-click, choose View Page Source, press Ctrl+F, and
search for a town you cover or a material you install. Not there? It isn’t in the page.

⚠️ Use View Page Source, not Inspect Element. Inspect shows the page after scripts have run, which
is what a person sees rather than what a crawler gets.

## The questions roofing customers actually ask AI

Roofing has a long, specific list, and most of it never appears on roofing websites:

- Who can come look at a roof leak today?
- How much does a new roof cost for a house my size?
- Will insurance cover this, and do you work with insurance companies?
- Do you do free inspections?
- How long does a roof replacement take?
- Do you offer financing?
- How long is the warranty, and what does it actually cover?
- Do you install metal, or just asphalt?
- Are you certified by a particular manufacturer?
- Do you cover my town?
- Are you licensed and insured, and do you carry workers’ comp?
- Can you tarp it today until you can do the repair?
- Do you subcontract the work or use your own crews?

You answer all of these on the phone, every week. Almost none of them are written down anywhere on
your website.

That isn’t a marketing failure — it’s an inventory problem. You have the answers. They’re just not
somewhere a machine can reach.

## Four roofing specifics worth getting right

**Insurance work.** This is the biggest single opportunity in the list. After a storm, an enormous
share of the questions are insurance questions, and most roofing websites say something vague like
“we work with all insurance companies.” Say the useful version instead: do you do the inspection
and documentation, do you meet the adjuster on site, do you handle supplements, do you require
payment before the claim settles. Those are the things that decide who gets called, and they’re
specific enough for an engine to quote.

**Manufacturer certifications.** If you’re a certified installer for a major manufacturer, say
which one, by name, in text. Customers ask by brand — especially when a warranty is involved — and
a named certification is exactly the kind of concrete, checkable fact an answer engine can use.
Don’t leave it in a logo image.

**Emergency response.** “Can you tarp it today” is a real question with a yes or no answer. If the
answer is yes, say so plainly with your actual response window. If it’s no, say that too.

**Price ranges.** You can’t quote a roof blind and nobody expects you to. But “most asphalt
replacements on a typical single-family home in this area run $9,000–$18,000 depending on size,
pitch, and layers” is far more useful — and far more quotable — than “contact us for a free quote.”
The vague version gives an engine nothing, and gives the customer a reason to call the company that
was upfront.

## Write answers the way an engine can use them

**Answer in the first sentence.** Not “For over 25 years, our family-owned company has proudly
protected homes…” Start with: “Yes — we do same-day emergency tarping across the county, usually
within four hours of the call.”

**Use real numbers, towns, and timeframes.** “Fast, reliable service” cannot be lifted into an
answer because it doesn’t say anything. “Most replacements are done in one to two days” can.

**Name your towns individually.** “The tri-state area” is vague. Listing actual towns means you’re
in the running when somebody asks about one by name.

**Say what you don’t do.** “We don’t do flat commercial roofs” or “we don’t handle solar removal
and reinstall” isn’t lost business. It stops you being recommended for jobs you’d turn down, and
that kind of specificity makes an engine more confident about the jobs you do want.

**Two to four sentences each.** Engines extract chunks. A tight chunk gets used; a long paragraph
gets skipped.

## Get it onto your own site as plain text

The answers have to live as real, crawlable HTML on your own domain. Not in a plugin, not in an
iframe, not only on a lead-generation directory.

Why your own domain matters here more than in most trades: roofing leads are expensive, and the
directories know it. If your only readable details live on someone else’s marketplace page, you’re
feeding their citation and paying for the lead twice.

Most builders have a native code block for this — WordPress has Custom HTML, Squarespace has Code,
Webflow has Embed. Wix is the exception worth flagging, because its embed tool wraps content in an
iframe that crawlers treat as a separate document; on Wix, use native text elements instead. We
walked through all four
[step by step here](/blog/how-to-add-html-to-wordpress-squarespace-webflow-or-wix).

Then run the View Page Source check again. If the answers show up in the raw source, it’s really
done.

## Don’t skip the foundations

None of this replaces the basics, and in roofing the basics carry real weight:

- Google Business Profile, claimed, accurate, with your true service area and the Q&A section on
  the profile filled in.
- Reviews — and reviews that mention the specific job and how fast you responded are worth several
  that say “great crew.”
- A site that works on a phone, because the leak search happens standing in a hallway looking at a
  ceiling stain.

Regular SEO built that foundation and it still is the foundation. AEO is the floor above it: making
sure that when an engine assembles an answer about roofing in your area, it has something specific
and true to say about your company.
