You shouldn’t need to learn a new discipline just to get found. And if you do have a marketing
person, this hands off cleanly.

Most tools in this space are built for people who do marketing all day. You open them and get a
dashboard with forty metrics, a keyword difficulty score, a crawl report, and a settings tab full of
things you’re apparently supposed to have an opinion about.

That’s fine if optimizing websites is your job. It isn’t yours. You run a plumbing company, or a
bakery, or a two-person software business. You’ve got maybe twenty minutes for this, somewhere
between payroll and a customer who needs to be called back.

So we built FaqFlo for you instead.

## What “for business owners” actually meant

It’s easy to say a product is simple. Here’s what it cost us to mean it.

**Plain questions, not keywords.** You don’t need to know what search volume is. FaqFlo finds the
actual questions people ask AI about businesses like yours — “who does emergency plumbing near me on
a Sunday,” not `emergency plumber denver [KD 34]`. You read them and think, yes, people ask me that
constantly.

**Answers, not a writing assignment.** The generator drafts answers built the way answer engines
like to quote them: direct first sentence, specific facts, no throat-clearing. You edit them the way
you’d correct an employee who got the details slightly wrong — because you’re the one who knows your
hours, your service area, and your actual prices.

**One score, not a report.** The audit tells you whether AI can see your site and whether you’re
getting cited today. That’s the number that matters. We resisted showing you the other thirty.

**Copy, paste, done.** You get a block of HTML. You paste it into your site where you’d paste
anything else — WordPress custom HTML block, Squarespace code block, Webflow embed. No plugin to
install, no account to connect, no permissions to grant. And critically, the content lives on your
domain, so when an AI cites the answer, the credit and the click come to you.

That last one is worth being blunt about: the popular alternative, a
[FAQ plugin](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea), renders its answers with JavaScript, and AI
crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript. Pretty on your page, invisible to the engines. Simple was
never the reason we skipped the widget — it just happened to be simpler and the only thing that
works.

## Simple on the outside, not simple underneath

Being easy to use is not the same as being lightweight.

Underneath, FaqFlo is doing real [answer engine optimization](/blog/what-is-aeo): figuring out
whether crawlers can read your site at all, structuring answers so they’re quotable, generating the
schema and the llms.txt file, then checking back over time to see whether you’re actually turning up
in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini.

That’s the part that would take a consultant a week to explain and a while longer to do. We didn’t
remove it. We just stopped making you look at it.

## And if you do have a marketing person — hand it over

Here’s the part people assume is a tradeoff and isn’t.

Owner-friendly usually means locked-down. Ours doesn’t. Everything FaqFlo produces is standard,
portable, and immediately useful to someone who does this professionally:

**The audit is a work order.** It says what’s wrong in specific terms — JS-rendered content, a
robots.txt blocking GPTBot or ClaudeBot, missing structured data. Forward it to your web developer
and they’ll know exactly what to do.

**The export is real HTML and JSON-LD.** Not a proprietary embed. Your agency can drop it into a
template, restyle it to match your brand, or wire it into your CMS without asking us for anything.

**The questions are a content brief.** The gap list — questions people ask that you haven’t answered
— is genuinely useful input for whoever writes your blog or runs your ads.

**The tracking is the report they’d have to build.** Citation data across the major answer engines,
over time, is the thing a marketer can take into a meeting.

So: run it yourself in an afternoon, or run it once and forward the output to your team, your
agency, or the person who “does the website.” Both are supported paths. Neither one requires you to
become a marketer, and neither one wastes a marketer’s time.

## The honest version

We think most small businesses are going to go uncited by AI for a while, not because the work is
hard, but because the tools that do it are built for a job title most small businesses don’t have.

FaqFlo is our attempt at the other thing: get in, see whether AI can find you, fix it, get on with
your day. And if you want to pass it to someone who lives in this stuff — the door’s open, and
everything’s in a format they can use.
