## What preferred sources actually is

Preferred sources is a Google setting that lets a signed-in person choose the websites they want to
see more of. Once someone picks your site, Google is more likely to put your content in front of
them — and only them.

Think of it like following someone on Instagram, except it happens inside Google Search. The person
is telling Google, “I trust this site. Show me more of it.”

Google says people have already picked more than 600,000 unique sources.

This isn’t brand new. Readers have been able to do this since 2025, and in May 2026 Google extended
it into AI answers. What’s new is how they do it.

## What changed on August 20

Before, a reader had to leave your page, go find a Google settings screen, search for your site, and
add it. That’s a lot of steps for someone who just wanted to read your post about water heaters.

On August 20, 2026, Google released an interactive “Preferred Sources” button that publishers can
put on their own pages. When a reader clicks it, it adds the site as a preferred source and sends
them straight back to where they were on your page.

So the whole thing now happens in one click, without the reader losing their place.

## What it does — and what it doesn’t

This is where a lot of the write-ups get sloppy, so here’s the careful version straight from
Google’s own documentation.

**In Top Stories:** your content is more likely to appear, marked with a “preferred” badge.

**In AI Mode and AI Overviews:** your content can be highlighted with a “preferred” badge for users
who have selected your site.

Read those two again. For Top Stories, Google promises your stuff shows up more often. For AI
answers, what Google documents is the badge — your link stands out for that reader. Those aren’t the
same promise, and anyone telling you a button gets you into AI answers more often is going further
than Google did.

**It is not a ranking boost.** Nothing here changes where you sit in search results for the general
public. It’s a per-person setting. A stranger who has never heard of you sees exactly what they saw
yesterday.

**The click covers your whole site.** Only domain-level and subdomain-level sites are eligible —
`yourdomain.com` and `blog.yourdomain.com` count, but `yourdomain.com/blog` does not. So one click
on one blog post covers everything you publish on that domain.

**About that “twice as likely” number.** You’ll see it everywhere: Google said prior studies showed
people were twice as likely to click through to a preferred source. It’s probably true, but keep
your head about it. That’s Google’s own number, and it’s measuring people who already liked you
enough to pick you. Of course they click more. It doesn’t mean the button doubles anyone’s traffic.

## Step one: check if you’re even eligible

Do this before you touch any code.

Go to `google.com/preferences/source` and type your domain into the search box. If your site shows
up there, you’re eligible.

If it doesn’t show up, adding the button won’t fix that. Google decides which sites appear in the
tool, and it hasn’t published exact rules. The common pattern people report is that sites publishing
fresh content with some consistency tend to be listed. If you haven’t posted in a year, that’s the
thing to fix first.

## The install

If you’re eligible, here’s all of it. First, this goes in your page’s `<head>`:

```
<script async src="https://news.google.com/swg/js/v1/publisher.js"></script>
```

Then this goes wherever you want the button to appear:

```
<div google-add-preferred-source-btn></div>
```

That’s it. Google styles the button and translates it into the reader’s language automatically. You
can add `data-theme="dark"` for a dark version.

Note what’s *not* there: no publisher ID, no account setup, no plugin. Some guides floating around
say you need a Publisher Center ID. Google’s official documentation doesn’t ask for one.

If your site builder won’t let you add scripts, Google supports a plain link instead. It points at
the same place and works in emails and social posts too:

```
<a href="https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=yourdomain.com">Add as Preferred Source</a>
```

Google also gives away official button images if you’d rather use a graphic than a text link.

## Where to put it

Put it where a reader has just gotten something out of you — the end of a blog post, not the top of
your homepage. A button at the top is asking for trust before you’ve earned it in that session.

Add one short line of text next to it, because most people have never seen this button before.
Something like: “Like this? Tell Google to show you more of our posts.”

And test it on your phone. That’s where most of your readers are.

## The honest limitation

Here’s the part worth sitting with.

This button only works on people who are already on your website. It’s a loyalty tool. It makes your
existing readers stickier, and that’s genuinely useful — but it does nothing for the customer who is
asking ChatGPT or Google which plumber to call and has never heard your name.

That customer is the bigger prize, and you don’t reach them with a button. You reach them by having
clear, factual content that answer engines can actually read and quote.

There’s a small irony in here too. Google’s preferred sources button is JavaScript, and that’s
completely fine — it’s a control for a human to click, not content for a machine to read. But it’s a
good reminder of the difference. Buttons can be JavaScript. Your answers can’t. Most FAQ plugins
render their answers with JavaScript, and AI crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript, so those
answers are invisible to the exact engines you’re trying to show up in.

A preferred sources button on a page that AI can’t read is a nice gesture on an empty room.

## What to actually do

1. Check whether your domain is listed in Google’s source preferences tool. Two minutes.
2. If you’re listed, add the two lines to your blog template. Ten minutes.
3. If you’re not listed, publish something. That’s the real work anyway.
4. Either way, make sure the content on your site is readable by AI in the first place.
