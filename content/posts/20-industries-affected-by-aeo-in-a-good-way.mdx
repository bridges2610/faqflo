## First, the good news

Most of what you read about AI and small business is about what you’re going to lose. Traffic.
Clicks. The top of the search results.

Here’s the part that gets skipped: when an AI answers a question, it names businesses. Two or three
of them. Someone asks “who should I call for a leaking roof in Nashua” and gets a short list of real
companies with a sentence about each.

That’s not a threat. That’s a recommendation, given to a stranger who is ready to buy, by a source
they trust. The only question is whose name is in it.

AEO — Answer Engine Optimization — is the work of making sure it’s yours. And it turns out that work
pays off more in some industries than others.

## What makes an industry a good fit for AEO

Three things, mostly.

**People ask questions before they buy.** Nobody asks an AI which brand of paper towels to get. They
absolutely ask which roofer to call, which dentist takes their insurance, and whether their heat pump
is worth repairing.

**The purchase is local or specific.** “Near me” questions force the AI to name actual businesses
instead of giving general advice.

**The answers are facts you already know.** Your service area, your hours, your pricing structure,
whether you do emergency calls. You don’t have to invent this. You just have to publish it in a form
a machine can read.

If your business ticks those boxes, you’re on the list below.

## A walkthrough: what this looks like for a roofer

Say you run a roofing company covering a few towns. Storm season hits, and somewhere in your service
area a homeowner sits down with a wet ceiling stain and types into ChatGPT: “Roof leaking after a
storm — who do I call in my area, and does insurance cover it?”

The AI answers in two parts. First it explains storm damage claims. Then it names two or three
roofers.

Here’s what decides whether you’re one of them.

**Before AEO.** Your website has a nice photo gallery, a contact form, and a paragraph about your
commitment to quality. Your FAQ page is
[a plugin that opens accordions](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) when you tap them — which
means the answers load with JavaScript, and AI crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript. To a person,
your site looks great. To the answer engine, that page is nearly blank. You aren’t rejected from the
answer. You’re just not in the running.

**After AEO.** The same questions your office manager answers on the phone twelve times a week are
now published as plain, crawlable text on your own domain:

- **Do you handle insurance claims?** “Yes — we document the damage, meet your adjuster on site, and
  bill the claim directly.”
- **How fast can you tarp a leaking roof?** “Same day in Nashua, Hudson, and Merrimack. Emergency
  tarping is a flat $450, credited toward the repair.”
- **How long does a full asphalt shingle replacement take?** “One to two days for a typical
  single-family home.”
- **Do you offer financing?** “Yes, 0% for 12 months on replacements over $8,000.”

That second version is full of the exact details an AI needs to make a recommendation: a service
area, a timeframe, a price, a specific offer. It’s also the kind of sentence an answer engine can
lift straight into a reply — “Northline Roofing offers same-day emergency tarping in the Nashua area
and bills insurance claims directly.”

Nothing about your business changed. What changed is that a machine could finally read it.

And notice the timing. That homeowner isn’t comparison shopping. There’s water coming through the
ceiling. Being one of two names in that answer is worth more than being the sixth result on a search
page — because that job is going to whoever gets called first.

## The 20 industries

Grouped by why AEO works there. Every one of these has customers who ask AI questions before they
pick up the phone.

### Home services — urgent, local, high ticket

**1. Roofing and exterior contractors.** Storm damage, insurance claims, “how much does a new roof
cost.” Urgent, expensive, and heavily researched — the strongest fit on this list.

**2. Plumbing.** “Who’s open on a Sunday” is a question with exactly one right kind of answer, and it
names a business.

**3. HVAC and heating oil.** Repair-or-replace questions, efficiency rebates, emergency no-heat calls
in January.

**4. Electricians.** Panel upgrades, EV charger installs, and “is this code” questions that reward a
specific, factual answer.

**5. Landscaping and lawn care.** Seasonal, recurring, and defined by service area — an AI can only
recommend you if it knows which towns you cover.

**6. Pest control.** People describe a problem (“small black ants in the kitchen”) and want a
solution plus a company. Your answers can be both.

**7. Cleaning services.** Pricing structure, what’s included, whether you bring supplies. All
questions, all publishable.

**8. Moving and storage.** High-consideration, one-shot purchases where people research hard and buy
once.

### Professional services — trust decided before the first call

**9. Law firms, especially solo and small practices.** Practice area plus location plus “do I even
have a case” is one of the most common AI question shapes there is.

**10. Accountants and bookkeepers.** Deadlines, entity types, what your monthly fee actually
includes.

**11. Insurance agents.** Coverage explanations are pure Q&A — the format is already right.

**12. Real estate agents and brokerages.** Local market questions, buying process questions, and “who
should I work with in this town.”

**13. Financial advisors.** Fee structure and minimums are exactly the facts people want before
they’ll book a call, and the ones most sites hide.

**14. IT services and managed service providers.** Small business buyers ask AI to explain the
problem and recommend a vendor in the same breath.

### Health and care — people research before they book

**15. Dental practices.** Insurance accepted, new patient availability, what a cleaning costs. This
is the most-asked category in local health.

**16. Veterinary clinics.** Urgent, emotional, and full of “is this an emergency” questions where
being the helpful answer wins the appointment.

**17. Physical therapy, chiropractic, and med spas.** Procedure explanations plus “who does this near
me.”

**18. Senior care and home health.** The person searching is usually an adult child, doing careful
research on someone else’s behalf, and reading everything.

### Products and everything else

**19. Restaurants, bakeries, and caterers.** Dietary questions, custom orders, lead times, private
events — all the things people ask an AI instead of calling to find out.

**20. E-commerce and solo software businesses.** Product comparison and “what’s the best tool for X”
questions are being answered by AI right now, and the answer names specific brands.

Honorable mentions that behave the same way: auto repair shops, fitness studios, childcare and
preschools, wedding and event vendors, salons, self-storage, and equipment rental.

## The pattern across all 20

Look at what those industries have in common. Not one of them is winning because of a big ad budget.

They’re winning because the questions their customers ask are answerable with facts they already
have. The service area. The price. The turnaround. The thing they explain on the phone every single
day.

AEO isn’t a new marketing discipline you have to learn. It’s mostly the act of writing those answers
down clearly and publishing them somewhere a machine can actually read — which means real, crawlable
HTML on your own domain, not an accordion widget that loads after the page does.

SEO still matters. It’s the foundation, and a healthy site helps you either way. AEO is the next
floor up: the layer that decides whether you’re the answer, not just a result.
