For years, FAQ schema had an obvious payoff you could see: add the markup, and your search listing
might sprout a little expandable list of questions. It took up space, pushed competitors down, and
made the case for itself.

Google retired those rich results. A great deal of advice still circulating hasn’t caught up, which
is why people keep installing plugins for a feature that no longer exists.

## So why keep the markup?

Because the listing was never the only thing it did. Schema is a small block of structured data
that removes guesswork for anything reading your page programmatically. It says: this text is a
question, this text is its answer, and this is the business they belong to.

Which matters more now than it did, not less:

- Answer engines lean on structure to work out what a page is actually claiming.
- It removes ambiguity about which business an answer belongs to.
- It costs nothing to serve and does not affect how your page looks.

## What it will not do

Schema is a label, not content. Marking up a thin answer does not make it a good one, and no amount
of structured data will get a page quoted if the page does not say anything worth quoting. The
markup helps a machine understand what you wrote — the writing is still the part that does the work.

Worth adding, then. Just for the right reason, and with no expectation of a listing that was
switched off. If you want both halves handled at once, [the generator](/#try) writes the answers
and the markup together.
