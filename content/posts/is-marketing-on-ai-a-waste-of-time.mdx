Some of it is. The part that works is smaller, simpler, and lasts longer than you’d think.

No, marketing to AI isn’t a waste of time. But a lot of what gets sold as “AI marketing” is.

The difference comes down to one question: are you giving AI something it can read and quote? If
yes, your time keeps paying you back. If no, you could work on it every week and never show up once.

## Why it feels like a waste

If you’ve tried to “do something about AI” and walked away frustrated, you’re not alone. There are
good reasons it feels pointless.

**You can’t see it working.** With an ad, you see clicks. With AI, someone asks ChatGPT a question,
gets your name, and calls you. Nothing shows up in your website stats. It feels like nothing
happened, even when it did.

**The advice is all over the place.** One person says post more. Another says add special code.
Another says it’s all hype. When nobody agrees, it’s hard to believe any of it.

**Someone promised you too much.** “Rank #1 in ChatGPT!” No one can promise that. AI tools decide
what they say. When a promise like that falls flat, it’s easy to decide the whole thing is fake.

All fair. But the problem usually isn’t AI. It’s what people are being told to do about it.

## Where the wasted time actually goes

Here’s the stuff that really is a waste of your time.

**Pumping out AI-written filler.** Twenty blog posts that say nothing won’t get you quoted. AI tools
are looking for a clear answer to a real question. Generic content is easy for them to skip.

**Chasing tricks.** Hidden text, stuffed keywords, “secret prompts.” These don’t build anything. At
best they do nothing. At worst they make your site look less trustworthy.

**Writing great answers AI can’t read.** This one hurts the most, because you did the work.
[Many FAQ plugins and page widgets](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) load their text with
JavaScript. AI crawlers usually don’t run JavaScript. So your visitors see your answers, and the AI
sees a blank spot. All that effort, invisible.

**Watching a scoreboard before you’ve played.** Checking whether AI mentions you is useful. But
tracking comes after the basics are fixed. Measuring a site AI can’t read just tells you the same
bad news every week.

## Where your time is well spent

The work that pays off is plain. It’s also shorter than most people expect.

**Answer the questions people actually ask.** “Do you do same-day service?” “How much does it cost?”
“Do you cover my town?” These are the exact questions people type into AI. Answer each one in a
sentence or two.

**Be specific.** “We care about quality” can’t be quoted. “Licensed electricians, same-day repairs in
Boise, no weekend surcharge” can. Names, places, prices, and hours are what AI picks up and repeats.

**Put it on your own website, in plain text.** Your answers need to be in the page itself, not
loaded in later by a widget. That way AI can read them, and when it quotes you, the credit points
back to your site.

**Keep your SEO basics in place.** A healthy website still matters a lot. Good page titles, a site
that loads fast, your business info kept up to date. That’s the foundation.
[AI visibility](/blog/what-is-aeo) is the next floor up, and it stands on top of it.

Here’s the good part. Once a clear answer is live on your site, it keeps working. A social post
fades in a day. An ad stops the minute you stop paying. A good answer that AI can read can get
quoted for months.

## How to tell if it’s working

You don’t need a fancy report to get a first read.

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google. Ask the questions a real customer would ask. “Who’s the best
dog groomer near Tulsa?” See whose names come back.

Then give it time. [AI tools don’t update overnight](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-ai-search-to-pick-up-your-content).
Think in weeks, not days.

It also helps to split “working” into two steps. First: can AI read your answers? That part you
control, and you can check it right away. Second: is AI quoting you? That part depends on the AI
tools, so it takes longer. Getting the first step right is what makes the second one possible.

## So, is it worth it?

If “AI marketing” means chasing tricks and posting filler, skip it. That’s a waste.

If it means writing clear answers to real questions and putting them where AI can read them, it’s
one of the better uses of an afternoon you’ll find. You do it once, fix it when things change, and
it keeps working while you run your business.
