Here’s a phone call you’ll never get.

Someone asked an AI assistant whether you’re open on Saturdays. You are. The AI said you close at
noon Friday and don’t reopen until Monday. So they called someone else, and you never found out it
happened.

That’s the part that makes this different from every other marketing problem you’ve had. A bad ad
you can measure. A slow website you can see. This one is completely silent.

## Why AI makes things up about small businesses

It helps to understand the mechanism, because it explains what to do about it.

An answer engine assembles its answer out of whatever it can find. Your website, your Google
Business Profile, directory listings, old cached pages, review sites, a local blog post from 2021.
It stitches those together into something that reads confidently.

Where it has good information, it’s usually accurate. Where it doesn’t, a few things go wrong:

**It uses stale data.** You changed your hours eighteen months ago. A directory listing still says
the old ones. That listing got crawled; your update maybe didn’t.

**It fills gaps with what’s typical.** If nothing readable says your hours, an engine can lean on
what businesses like yours usually do. Most bakeries close at 3pm, so it says 3pm. You close at 6.

**It blends you with a similar business.** Same industry, same town, similar name. The details get
crossed. This happens more than you’d think with common business names.

**It reads the wrong part of your site.** Your hours are in a nice graphic in the footer. The
crawler can’t read images, but it can read the line on your Contact page that says “holiday hours
may vary” — so that’s what it uses.

And critically: it never says “I don’t know.” These systems are built to produce an answer. Silence
isn’t an option they take. When they can’t find your facts, they don’t leave you out with an
apology — they produce something plausible.

## It isn’t just hours

Hours are the easiest example, but the same failure hits everything a customer needs to know
before calling:

- **Prices** — often quoted from an old page or a competitor’s range
- **Service area** — towns you cover, towns you don’t
- **Services you don’t offer** — getting recommended for a job you’d turn down wastes everyone’s
  time and burns a lead
- **Whether you’re still open at all** — permanently-closed flags on stale listings are brutal
- **Booking requirements** — “walk-ins welcome” when you’re appointment-only
- **Phone number** — an old one from a listing you forgot existed

Each of these is a customer who either doesn’t call, or calls and is annoyed before you say hello.

## How to find out what AI says about you

Twenty minutes. Do this today.

1. Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google (look at the AI answer at the top). Optionally add
   Claude and Copilot.
2. Ask each one, plainly: *“What are the hours for [your business name] in [your town]?”*
3. Then: *“What does [your business] charge for [common service]?”*
4. Then: *“Does [your business] serve [a town you cover]?”*
5. Then: *“Is [your business] still open?”*
6. Write down every answer, especially the wrong ones.

Don’t argue with the AI or try to correct it in the chat — that doesn’t stick and doesn’t affect
what anyone else sees. You’re gathering evidence, not negotiating.

Ask again from a signed-out or private browser window if you can, so you’re seeing something
closer to what a stranger sees.

Doing this once is genuinely worth twenty minutes — you’ll learn more about how you look to AI

## How to actually fix it

You can’t edit the AI. You can change what it reads. That’s the whole strategy, and it works in a
specific order.

### 1. Fix your Google Business Profile first

It’s the single most-used source for local business facts, and it’s the fastest thing on this list.
Claim it, correct the hours, set special hours for holidays, confirm the service area, correct the
phone number, and fill in the Q&A section on the profile.

### 2. Put the facts on your own site as plain text

Not in an image. Not in a PDF. Not in a JavaScript widget that loads after the page does — AI
crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript, so those answers aren’t there for them.

Write the facts out flatly:

> **What are your hours?** We’re open Monday to Friday, 7am to 6pm, and Saturday 8am to 2pm. We’re
> closed Sundays, and we run emergency service 24/7 for existing customers.

That’s the kind of sentence an engine can lift straight into an answer.

### 3. Clean up the stale listings

Yelp, Angi, Bing Places, industry directories, that Chamber of Commerce page from 2019. Find the
ones with old information and update or remove them. Search your own phone number to find the ones
you forgot about.

### 4. Be consistent everywhere

Same business name, same address format, same phone, same hours, in every place. Inconsistency is
where the confusion starts — if three sources disagree, the engine picks one, and it may not pick
yours.

### 5. Then check again in a few weeks

Engines re-crawl on their own schedule. Fixes generally show up in weeks, not days, and not all
engines update at the same pace.

## The part worth sitting with

None of this is exotic. It’s the same principle that’s always governed being found online: if it
isn’t written down somewhere a machine can read it, it doesn’t exist. Traditional SEO taught that
lesson about search rankings. Answer Engine Optimization is the same lesson, one floor up, with
higher stakes — because a search result that’s missing your hours just sends someone to your
website, while an AI answer with the wrong hours sends them to your competitor and tells them it’s
your fault.

The good news is that your business facts are the easiest content you’ll ever write. You already
know all of it. It just has to be somewhere readable, in plain sentences, on a page you control.
