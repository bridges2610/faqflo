For most of the last two decades, getting found online meant one thing: rank on Google, earn the
click, win the customer. Simple enough that an entire industry grew up around the middle step.

Then people started asking instead of searching. “What’s the best bookkeeper near me?” “Is this
company legit?” And rather than ten blue links, they get one paragraph with a couple of sources
named underneath it.

## The shortlist got shorter

A results page was a list you could work your way down. If you ranked fourth, someone scrolling
still found you. An AI answer isn’t a list — it names two or three sources, and everyone else is
simply absent from the conversation.

> There is no “you lost this one” notification. They just went with whoever got quoted.

That is what makes this hard to notice. A ranking drop shows up in a report. Not being mentioned in
someone’s ChatGPT answer shows up nowhere at all — no impression, no bounce, no line on a
dashboard. The first signal is usually a quiet quarter.

## What still works

Reassuringly, most of it. Being genuinely useful, answering real questions plainly, and having a
site that loads and can be read all still matter. What changed is the format that gets rewarded: a
clear question with a direct answer under it, in text, on a page a machine can read.

Which is a long way of saying the work is more like writing and less like chasing backlinks — a
change most small businesses should welcome. There is more on how the pieces fit together in
[the AI SEO guide](/seo-guide).
