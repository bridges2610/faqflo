An honest look at what still works, what’s a treadmill, and the three things to do if marketing just
isn’t your thing.

<PostTldr>
Marketing is splitting into two kinds: things you have to keep feeding (social, ads, trends) and
things that pay you back for years (your website, your answers, your reviews, your customer list).

- Fewer people click through to websites now. They get answers from AI, maps, and short video — so
  being named and trusted matters more than being ranked.
- If marketing isn’t your thing, do three things: make your business readable, ask every customer for
  a review, and keep your own list. Everything else is optional.

</PostTldr>

Here’s the honest version: most marketing advice you’ll read this year is written by people who sell
marketing. So it’s going to tell you to do everything. Post daily. Run ads. Start a podcast. Learn
the new AI tool. Be on the new app.

You can’t do all of that. You have a business to run. So let’s sort marketing into what actually
moves the needle in 2027 and beyond, what’s fine but optional, and what’s a treadmill you can step
off.

## The big shift: people get answers, not links

For twenty years, marketing meant getting the click. You showed up in a search result, someone
clicked, they landed on your page, and you sold them from there.

That path is thinning out. SparkToro’s 2026 analysis found **about 68% of Google searches now end
without a click** — up from roughly 60% two years earlier, and higher still on a phone. Someone asks
an AI assistant or sees a Google AI answer, and gets what they need right there. They ask for “a good
electrician near me who does same-day work” and they get two names and a sentence about each.

This doesn’t mean [websites are dead](/blog/does-a-website-matter-anymore-in-2026). It means the job
of your website changed. It used to be a storefront. Now it’s also the source material that AI reads
before it decides whether to recommend you. If it can’t read you, it recommends someone else.

Two more things changed at the same time:

**Attention is split into smaller pieces.** People are on more platforms, for shorter stretches. No
single channel reaches everyone anymore.

**Generic content is worthless.** AI can write a passable blog post in ten seconds, and everyone
knows it. So the internet is flooding with average content. What stands out now is specific content:
your real prices, your real service area, your real answers, your real customers saying real things.

Keep those three shifts in mind and the rest of this gets simple.

## Two kinds of marketing: what you rent, and what you own

The most useful way to think about marketing in 2027 is not by channel. It’s by whether the work
keeps paying you after you stop.

**Things you rent.** These work while you feed them and stop the moment you don’t. Social media
posts. Paid ads. Chasing trends. They can be very effective. They just never build up. Miss a month
and you’re back to zero.

**Things you own.** These compound. A good website with clear answers keeps getting found. A review
from 2024 is still working for you today. A customer list you’ve built for five years is worth more
every year. This is marketing equity — work that builds on itself.

Most small businesses spend their time on the rented stuff, because it’s what everyone talks about
and it gives you a quick hit of likes. Then they wonder why they have to keep running just to stay in
place.

Flip the ratio. Spend most of your limited time on what you own. Do the rented stuff when you can,
without guilt when you can’t.

## An honest scorecard, channel by channel

**Your website and SEO — still the foundation.** Don’t let anyone tell you SEO is over. It’s the
ground floor. A fast, clear site with real information about what you do and where you do it is what
everything else sits on. What’s changed: fewer clicks arrive from search, so your site has to work
even when nobody clicks. That means the answers have to be on the page in plain text, not buried in a
design.

**[AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) (answer engine optimization) — the next floor up.** This is the new one,
and it’s real. AEO means writing your content so AI tools can read it, trust it, and quote you by
name when someone asks. It’s not a replacement for SEO. It’s what you build on top of it. The good
news is that it’s mostly about plain, specific, question-and-answer content on your own site — which
you probably should have had anyway.

**Reviews — the highest return of anything on this list.** Reviews are the closest thing to a cheat
code. They drive local search and maps. They’re [what AI reads](/blog/does-ai-use-my-google-reviews-how-reviews-affect-ai-recommendations)
to decide if you’re trustworthy. They’re what a customer checks after AI hands them your name. And
they compound forever. One habit — asking every happy customer — beats almost any campaign.

**Email and text — the list is equity, the sending is a treadmill.** Your list of past customers is
one of the most valuable things you own. Nobody can take it away, change an algorithm on it, or
charge you rent to reach it. It is also the most reliable hedge there is against a search result that
never sends a click. The honest catch: a list only works if you use it. A useful note every month or
two keeps you in mind when they need you again.

**Social media — keep up, but don’t depend on it.** Here’s the part people don’t like to hear.
Organic reach on most platforms is small now. A post reaches a fraction of your own followers. Viral
rarely turns into revenue. Social is still worth it for two reasons: it’s the first place many people
check to see if you’re real and active, and it’s where word of mouth happens in public. So keep it
alive. Post when you have something to show. Just don’t build your business on it, and don’t let it
eat your week.

**Paid ads — they work, and they cost more every year.** Ads are the fastest way to get customers and
the least durable. Costs rise as more businesses pile in. Ads are great for filling a slow month or
launching something new. They are a bad place to put your only money, because the leads stop the day
the budget does.

**Short-form video — powerful, expensive to keep up.** Video reaches people who never read. But it
demands constant new material. If you like doing it, do it. If you don’t, don’t guilt yourself into
it. There’s no evidence a plumber who hates being on camera needs to be.

**Word of mouth, referrals, and local partnerships — old, boring, undefeated.** The best lead you’ll
get this year will come from someone saying your name to someone else. That’s not a tactic. It’s a
result. It comes from doing good work, asking for reviews, and staying in touch. Everything above
feeds into this one.

## What doesn’t change

Underneath every shift, the same things win: be easy to find, be easy to understand, be easy to
trust, and be easy to contact.

AI didn’t change that. It just changed who’s asking. Now it’s often a machine asking on someone’s
behalf. It wants the same things a person wants: clear answers, real specifics, and proof from other
people that you’re good.

## If marketing isn’t your thing: the only three that matter

If you read nothing else, do these three. They’re all things you own, and all three keep working
while you’re busy running the business.

1. **Make your business readable.** One real website with the answers to the questions people
   actually ask — what you do, where, what it costs, how fast, what makes you different. Written in
   plain text an AI can read, not hidden in a widget or a PDF. This one step covers SEO, AEO, and
   most of what an AI needs to recommend you. Do it once, update it a few times a year.
2. **Ask every happy customer for a review.** Make it a habit, not a campaign. A text with a link,
   sent the day the job is done. That’s it. Nothing else on this list has a higher return for the
   effort.
3. **Keep your own list, and use it.** Collect emails or phone numbers from every customer. Send
   something useful every month or two — a tip, a seasonal reminder, a heads-up about availability.
   You’re not selling. You’re staying in mind.

That’s the whole plan. Social, ads, and video go on top of this if you have the time or the taste for
them. They don’t replace it.
