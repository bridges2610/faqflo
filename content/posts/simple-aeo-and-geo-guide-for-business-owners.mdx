## First, the two words

AEO stands for Answer Engine Optimization. GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization. Two
groups of people picked two names for the same job. If someone tries to sell you on the difference
between them, they’re selling you something.

Both mean this: when a person types a question into ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI, you want
the AI to name you.

You already know its older cousin. It’s called SEO, and it’s about getting your website onto page
one of Google. SEO is still the foundation — a healthy, findable website is what AEO is built on
top of. [AEO](/#aeo) is the next floor up. Instead of only trying to be a link on a list, you’re
also trying to be the answer.

## An example, start to finish

Maria owns a dog grooming shop in Portland.

Someone who just moved to town picks up their phone and types this into ChatGPT:

> Where can I get my dog groomed in Portland if he’s scared of clippers?

The AI writes back a short paragraph. It names two shops and says a little about each one. The
person picks one, calls, and books.

Maria was not one of the two names. Not because her shop is worse — she’s been doing this for
eleven years and she is genuinely great with nervous dogs. She wasn’t named because nothing on the
internet told the AI so.

Her website says “Portland’s friendliest groomers. Book today!” That’s a slogan. An AI can’t do
anything with a slogan. It needs facts.

Now here’s the same shop after one afternoon of work. Maria adds a section to her site that says,
in plain words:

> **Do you groom dogs that are scared of clippers?** Yes. We use quiet, low-vibration clippers and
> hand scissors for anxious dogs, and we book them into our quiet morning slots. No sedation. We’re
> in Portland, Oregon, and we’ve been doing this for 11 years.

Next time someone asks that question, the AI has something real to grab. And when it names her
shop, it can explain why — because she told it why.

That’s AEO. That’s GEO. That’s the whole job.

## Why most businesses get skipped

Three reasons. All fixable.

**Their website doesn’t answer anything.** Most small business sites have a home page, an about
page, and a contact form. Lots of adjectives. Almost no answers. An AI can’t quote an adjective.

**Their answers are too fuzzy.** “Great service at fair prices” tells an AI nothing. “Oil change,
$65, most cars, no appointment, open till 6” tells it everything it needs.

**The AI can’t actually read the page.** This is the sneaky one. Lots of websites use something
called JavaScript to make answers pop open when you click them — those little plus signs on a FAQ
page. Your visitors see the answers fine. But AI crawlers usually don’t click anything and don’t
run JavaScript. They grab the plain text of the page and leave. If your answers only show up after
a click, the AI reads a page with no answers on it.

That’s the part that stings. The page looks perfect. It’s just invisible to the exact robots you’re
trying to reach. (There’s a whole post on
[why FAQ plugins cause this](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) if you want the detail.)

## The five steps

Here’s the whole playbook. You can do it yourself.

**1. Write down the questions people actually ask you.** Not marketing topics. Real questions, in
the words a customer would use. “Do you come out on weekends?” “How much for a small dog?” “Do you
take walk-ins?” You already answer these on the phone ten times a week.

**2. Answer each one in the first sentence.** Don’t warm up. Don’t tell the story of your company.
Question, then answer, immediately. AI grabs the cleanest answer it can find, so make yours the
easiest one to grab. Two or three sentences is plenty.

**3. Put in the boring details.** Prices. Towns you serve. Hours. How fast you can get there. What
you don’t do. Boring is good here. Boring is specific, and specific is what gets quoted.

**4. Make sure it’s readable text on your own website.** Plain text, on your page, no clicking
required. On your own web address — not on somebody else’s platform — so when the AI names you, the
credit and the click come to you.

**5. Check whether it worked.** Ask ChatGPT and Google’s AI a few of your own customers’ questions
and see whose names come back. Do it again in a month. Names move around as businesses add content,
so this isn’t a one-time chore.

That’s it. There’s no trick. There’s no secret word you sprinkle in. It’s just answering plainly and
making sure a machine can read it.

## The one-minute version of the example

Before:

> We’re passionate about pet care and treat every dog like family.

After:

> **How much is a full groom for a small dog?** A full groom for a dog under 25 pounds is $75 and
> takes about 90 minutes. That includes bath, haircut, nails, and ears. Walk-ins welcome before
> 11am.

The first one is nice. The second one is quotable. Guess which one ends up inside an AI answer.
