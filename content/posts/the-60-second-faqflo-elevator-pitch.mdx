If you’ve got a minute between a customer call and payroll, this is the whole thing.

Most software gets explained to you in a language you didn’t sign up to learn. Somebody says
“structured data layer” and you nod and decide to deal with it later, and later never comes.

So here’s FaqFlo in sixty seconds, in plain English, for someone who runs a business and
[does not do marketing for a living](/blog/faqflo-for-business-owners).

## The pitch

Your customers are asking AI who to hire.

They open ChatGPT or Google, type “who’s good for X near me,” and get back two or three names. They
call one of them.

Right now, that name probably isn’t yours — not because you’re not good, but because the AI can’t
find anything readable about you. When it can’t find you, it doesn’t say “I’m not sure.” It
confidently names somebody else.

FaqFlo fixes that. It finds the questions people actually ask AI about businesses like yours. It
writes answers in the format AI likes to quote — direct, factual, specific. It hands you a block of
content you paste onto your own website in about two minutes. Then it checks back over time and
tells you whether you’ve started showing up.

That’s it. No plugin. No agency. No new vocabulary. Your website, your name, your credit.

That’s the minute. If you’re still here, the rest of this is the part you’d ask about over coffee.

## “Wait — my competitors are getting recommended and I’m not?”

Probably, yes. And it has almost nothing to do with who’s better at the actual job.

An AI builds a recommendation out of whatever it can read about a business. Services, service area,
pricing, hours, whether you do same-day. If that stuff exists somewhere in plain text it can crawl,
you’re a candidate. If it doesn’t, you’re not in the running — no matter how good your work is or
how long you’ve been at it.

The frustrating version of this is the business that has all the answers on their site and still
doesn’t get named. Which brings us to the trap.

## The trap almost everyone falls into

If you already have a FAQ section on your site, there’s a decent chance it’s invisible to AI.

Most [FAQ plugins and accordion widgets](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) build their
answers with JavaScript. A human opens the page, their browser runs the JavaScript, the answers
appear, everything looks great. AI crawlers generally don’t do that — they read the raw page and
move on. So your answers are there for people and gone for machines.

Great-looking FAQ. Zero citations. This is the single most common reason a business that “already
did this” still isn’t showing up.

## What you actually do, step by step

Four things, and only two of them involve you.

**1. Find out where you stand.** Run your website through [the audit](/#audit). It tells you whether
AI can see your site at all and whether you’re getting cited today. One number, not a thirty-page
report.

**2. Get your questions and answers.** FaqFlo pulls the real questions people ask AI about your
category and your area — “do you do emergency calls on Sunday,” “what does it cost,” “do you cover
my town.” Then it drafts answers built to be quoted. You read them and fix the details, because
you’re the one who knows your actual hours and prices.

**3. Paste it on your site.** You get a block of clean HTML. It goes into your site the same place
you’d paste anything else — the WordPress custom HTML block, the Squarespace code block, the Webflow
embed. No plugin to install, no account to connect, no permissions to grant.

**4. Watch whether it works.** FaqFlo checks
[ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google’s AI answers](/blog/ranking-the-best-ai-platforms-for-your-business)
over time and tells you which questions you’re getting named for and which ones you’re still
missing. When a gap shows up, it tells you what to answer next.

Steps 2 and 3 take an afternoon at most. Step 4 happens without you.

## Why it goes on your website

This is worth being stubborn about.

Some tools would happily host your content on their domain. That’s easier for them and worse for you
— when the AI cites the answer, the credit and the click go to them, not you.

FaqFlo puts the content on your domain. When an [answer engine](/blog/what-is-aeo) quotes it, your
business is the source. That’s the entire point.

## “I have someone who does my website”

Then hand it to them. Nothing here is locked up or proprietary.

The audit reads like a work order — it names what’s wrong in terms a developer understands. The
output is standard HTML and JSON-LD they can restyle or drop into a template. The question list is a
genuinely useful content brief. And the citation tracking is the report a marketer would otherwise
have to build by hand.

Run it yourself, or run it once and forward the output. Both work.

## The honest limits

Two things this isn’t.

It isn’t instant. AI answers shift over weeks, not hours. You’re building a position, not flipping a
switch. That’s also why the tracking matters — it’s the only way to know it’s working.

It isn’t a substitute for being good. A customer who gets your name from an AI will still glance at
your reviews and your site before calling. AI referrals extend your reputation; they don’t replace
it.

## The one-sentence version

If you only remember one thing: an AI can only recommend a business it can read, and right now it
probably can’t read yours.

FaqFlo is the fastest way to change that answer —
[and then to check that it stayed changed](/#audit).
