Most businesses are missing from AI answers because the AI literally can’t read their site — not
because the business is bad.

- The fixes are mostly boring: crawlable text, open doors for crawlers, direct answers to real

- The other half is proof. If nothing outside your own site vouches for you — reviews, a live
  profile, mentions you didn’t write — AI has no reason to put your name forward.
- You can’t fix what you haven’t measured. Check whether you’re cited today, then work down this
  list.

</PostTldr>

You’ve got a decent website. Reviews are good. Google still sends you some traffic. And yet when
someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI “who’s the best [what you do] near [where you are],”
your name never comes up.

It’s rarely one dramatic thing. It’s usually three or four small things stacked on top of each other,
and the AI quietly gives up and recommends someone else.

Here are the ten we see most, roughly in order of how often they’re the culprit.

## 1. Your answers are hidden behind JavaScript

This is the big one. Most [FAQ plugins, accordion widgets](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea),
and “smart” content blocks load their text with JavaScript after the page opens. Your visitors see it
fine. AI crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript — they read the raw HTML and move on. So the exact
answers you’d want quoted are, to them, a blank space.

**Fix:** Your questions and answers need to be plain text in the page the moment it loads. View your
page source (right-click → View Page Source) and search for one of your FAQ answers. If it’s not
there, the AI can’t see it either.

## 2. You’ve locked the AI crawlers out

Your robots.txt file tells crawlers which ones are welcome.
[A lot of themes, security plugins, and hosting defaults](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt) now
block AI crawlers — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended — without telling you. You may
have opted out of AI search without ever choosing to.

**Fix:** Open `yourdomain.com/robots.txt` and look for those names next to “Disallow.” If you want to
be cited, they need to be allowed in.

## 3. The basics aren’t on the page

“What does it cost?” “Do you cover my town?” “Are you open Saturdays?” If your site says “contact us
for a quote” and “serving the greater area,” the AI has nothing to work with. It can’t recommend a
plumber in Dover if it can’t confirm you work in Dover.

**Fix:** Put the plain facts in plain text: services, service area by town name, hours, starting
prices or price ranges, response times. Specific beats impressive every time.

## 4. You bury the answer

A lot of pages start with a story, a mission statement, or three paragraphs about your founding year
before they get to the point. Answer engines pull the cleanest, most direct sentence they can find.
If yours is in paragraph four, they’ll take someone else’s paragraph one.

**Fix:** Lead with the answer. First sentence, 30–60 words, then the explanation. Think “Yes, we do
same-day water heater replacement in Portsmouth” — not “For over 20 years, we’ve been committed
to…”

## 5. Your content isn’t shaped like a question

People type questions into AI. Your site is written in headings like “Our Services” and “About Us.”
There’s a mismatch, and the AI has to do the work of connecting the two. Often it doesn’t bother.

**Fix:** Add real question-and-answer content. This is why
[FAQ pages, done right](/blog/what-questions-should-be-on-your-faq-page), are one of the best AEO
tools you have — they’re already shaped like what people ask.

## 6. You’re answering the wrong questions

You have an FAQ, but it’s the questions *you* want to answer: “What makes us different?” “Why choose
us?” Meanwhile people are asking “Do you charge for estimates?” and “Can you come out tonight?”

**Fix:** Start from what people actually ask AI about businesses like yours in your area, and answer
those. If you don’t know what those questions are, that’s the first thing to find out.

## 7. Your SEO foundation is shaky

[AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) isn’t a replacement for SEO — it’s the next floor up. If your site is slow,
has thin pages, missing titles, broken links, or hasn’t been touched since 2019, answer engines treat
it the same way Google does: as something not worth trusting.

**Fix:** Get the basics right first. Clear page titles, a real page for each service, a working
mobile site, no dead ends. Then build the AEO layer on top.

## 8. Your best info lives somewhere AI can’t use it

Your price list is a PDF. Your hours are in an image. Your latest work is only on Instagram. Your FAQ
is embedded from another service inside an iframe. All of that looks fine to a human and reads as
nothing — or as someone else’s content — to a crawler.

**Fix:** Anything you want quoted should be plain HTML text on a page you own. If it’s in a PDF, an
image, a social post, or an embed from another domain, copy the important parts onto your site as
text.

## 9. There’s nothing telling AI you can be trusted

Everything above is about what your own site says. This one is about whether anybody else agrees.

An answer engine is about to put your name in front of a stranger and, in effect, vouch for you. It
would rather do that for a business the web corroborates than one that only vouches for itself. Your
site claiming you’re the best roofer in town is an assertion. Forty recent
[Google reviews](/blog/does-ai-use-my-google-reviews-how-reviews-affect-ai-recommendations), an
active profile, a mention in a local news piece, and a supplier listing you as an approved installer
are evidence.

So the question isn’t just “can AI read me?” It’s “if AI goes looking for a second opinion about me,
does it find one?” For a lot of small businesses the honest answer is no — and a competitor with
half the craftsmanship but a visible footprint gets named instead.

The signals that matter most:

- **Reviews, recent and plentiful.** Volume, rating and how fresh they are. Four reviews from 2022
  reads as a business that might not be trading any more.
- **A profile that looks alive.** Your Google Business Profile filled in and current — hours,
  services, photos, answered questions. Social accounts that have been posted to this year.
- **Mentions you didn’t write.** Local press, suppliers, trade associations, community pages,
  directories. [Anywhere your name appears](/blog/what-else-influences-companies-being-mentioned-in-ai)
  next to what you do, that you don’t control.
- **Consistency across all of it.** If your name is spelled three ways, your address is stale on two
  listings, and your hours disagree, every one of those signals gets weaker — because now the web
  doesn’t agree with itself either.

**Fix:** Ask every happy customer for a review, the day the job is done — it’s the highest-return
habit on this whole list. Fill in your Google Business Profile properly and keep it current. Get
listed wherever your trade lists people, and make your name, address, phone and hours identical
everywhere. This is the same work that has always helped local SEO. It just happens to be what an AI
reads to decide whether you’re a safe recommendation.

## 10. You’ve never actually checked

Most businesses have no idea whether they’re cited today, for what, or who’s being cited instead. So
nothing gets fixed, because nothing looks broken.

**Fix:** Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini five questions a real customer would ask. Write down who
comes up. Then do it again next month. Better yet, track it automatically, because the answers change
as your competitors’ content changes.

## Where to start

Don’t try to fix all ten. Check the first two — can AI read your site, and are crawlers allowed in?
If either fails, nothing else on this list matters yet. Once the doors are open, work down: real
questions, direct answers, plain text on your own domain.
