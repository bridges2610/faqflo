If you’ve been anywhere near a marketing newsletter lately, someone has told you your site needs an
llms.txt file. Usually with some urgency. Sometimes with a link to buy a generator.

So let’s do this plainly. What it is, what it does, and whether you should care — including the part
most articles about it leave out.

## What it is

llms.txt is a plain text file you put at the root of your website, like `yoursite.com/llms.txt`.
Inside, you write a short, human-readable map of your site: what your business is, and links to the
pages that matter most, with a line describing each.

That’s it. It’s a text file. You can write one in a notepad app.

The idea is straightforward. AI assistants have a limited window of attention. Your website is full
of navigation, cookie banners, footers, and menus that mean nothing to a machine. llms.txt is a way
of saying: here’s the signal, skip the noise.

If you’ve ever seen a robots.txt file, it’s a cousin of that. But robots.txt tells crawlers what
they *can’t* touch. llms.txt suggests what they *should* pay attention to.

A simple one looks roughly like this:

```
# Northline Roofing

> Residential roofing and emergency repair serving the Boise metro area.

## Key pages
- [Services](https://example.com/services): Roof repair, replacement, and same-day tarping.
- [Service area](https://example.com/areas): Towns and neighborhoods we cover.
- [FAQ](https://example.com/faq): Pricing, timelines, insurance, and warranty questions.
```

Clean, short, obvious.

## The honest verdict: it probably isn’t doing much yet

Here’s the part the hype skips.

llms.txt is a proposal, not a standard. It was suggested by a developer in 2024 and picked up
quickly among people who build with AI. What it does not have is confirmed, widespread support from
the big answer engines. Google has publicly been lukewarm at best. The major assistants have not
committed to reading it. There’s no verified case of a small business getting cited because of an
llms.txt file.

So when someone tells you this file is why you’re invisible to AI, they’re overselling it.

But — and this matters — it costs you about fifteen minutes and it can’t hurt. It’s a text file. It
doesn’t slow your site down, it doesn’t break anything, and if support does firm up, you’re already
done. That’s a reasonable bet at that price.

Just don’t confuse it with the thing that actually matters.

## What actually matters more

If you only have an hour to spend on being found by AI, do these first, in this order.

1. **Make sure your content is readable without JavaScript.** This is the big one and it sinks more
   small businesses than anything else. Most
   [FAQ plugins and accordion widgets](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) build their answers
   with JavaScript after the page loads. AI crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript — they grab the
   raw HTML and move on. So your best content looks perfect to visitors and simply doesn’t exist to
   the engines. An llms.txt pointing at a page the crawler can’t read is a map to an empty room.

2. **Make sure crawlers are allowed in.** [Check your robots.txt](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt).
   A surprising number of sites block GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or Google-Extended without
   the owner ever deciding to — it came with the theme, or a developer added it years ago as a
   blanket rule.

3. **Actually answer the questions.** Prices, service area, hours, timelines, what you don’t do. In
   plain sentences, on your own domain. This is the raw material an AI builds a recommendation out
   of. No file format substitutes for having something to say.

4. **Then, sure, write an llms.txt.** Fifteen minutes. Cheap insurance.

Notice that the first three are just good website hygiene. That’s the theme here. Traditional SEO
built the foundation — a healthy, crawlable, well-organized site.
[Answer Engine Optimization](/blog/what-is-aeo) is the next floor up, and it’s built out of the same
materials. There isn’t a magic file that skips the construction.

## How to write one, if you want to

Keep it short and specific.

- Start with your business name as a heading, then one line saying what you do and where.
- List 5–10 links to your genuinely most important pages, each with a short plain-English
  description.
- Use your real service area and real specialties in the wording — not marketing language.
- Save it as `llms.txt` and upload it to the root of your site, so it loads at
  `yoursite.com/llms.txt`.
- Check it loads in a browser. If you get a 404, it’s in the wrong folder.

Don’t stuff it with keywords, don’t list every page you have, and don’t describe pages as more
important than they are. The value of the file is that it’s honest and short.
