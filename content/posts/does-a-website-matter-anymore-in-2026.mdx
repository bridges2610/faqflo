Yes, a website still matters in 2026. But it matters for a different reason than it used to.

It used to be the place customers landed. Now it’s more often the place AI goes to learn about you
before it answers a customer’s question. Fewer people may visit your site. More software is reading
it on their behalf.

If you’ve been wondering whether to let your website go stale, that’s the part worth knowing before
you do.

## Why people are asking this question

The doubt makes sense. Here’s what a lot of business owners are seeing:

Traffic is flat or dropping. More customers call after “asking ChatGPT” or reading a Google AI
answer. Plenty of new jobs come in through a Facebook message or a Google Business Profile, and
nobody seems to click the website link at all.

So it’s fair to ask: if people get their answer without visiting, why pay for a website?

The answer is that the visit was never the whole point. The website’s real job was always to tell the
world what you do, where you do it, and why you’re a good choice. That job didn’t go away. The reader
changed.

## Your website’s new job: be the source

When someone asks an AI “who does same-day furnace repair near me,” the AI doesn’t make up an answer.
It builds one from what it can find and read about local businesses.

That’s where your website comes in.

Your website is the one place on the internet where you decide what it says. Your services. Your
service area. Your hours. Your prices. What makes you different. Written in your words, as detailed
as you want.

An AI that can read all of that has something solid to work with. It can say “they come out the same
day and don’t charge extra on weekends,” because you told it so.

An AI that can’t find that has to guess, or skip you. And when it skips you, the customer doesn’t go
without an answer. They get your competitor’s name instead.

## “Can’t I just use Facebook and Google?”

Keep them. They help. Reviews, your Google Business Profile, and directory listings all feed into
what AI knows about you.

But they have limits your website doesn’t:

**You don’t own them.** The platform sets the rules, the layout, and what shows up. That can change
any time.

**They hold less.** A profile has a few fixed boxes. It usually can’t hold a clear answer to “do you
work on older oil furnaces?” or “what does a service call cost?”

**Some of it is hard for AI to read.** A lot of social media content sits behind a login or loads in
ways crawlers can’t see. What’s easy for a person to scroll isn’t always easy for a machine to read.

Think of it this way. Your profiles are what other places say about you. Your website is your own
statement. AI uses both, but only one of them is fully up to you.

## It’s also where customers double-check you

Even when AI names you, many people take one more step before they call. They look you up.

If they find a clear, current website, that confirms the recommendation. If they find nothing, a
broken page, or [hours from 2019](/blog/ai-is-telling-customers-the-wrong-hours-about-your-business),
they go back and try the next name on the list.

So your website now has two readers. The AI, which decides whether to mention you. And the customer,
who decides whether to trust that mention. Both need to find the same clear story.

## What a website needs to do now

The good news: this doesn’t mean you need a bigger or fancier site. In most cases, you need a clearer
one.

**Answer the questions people actually ask.** “Do you do same-day?” “What does it cost?” “Do you
cover my town?” Put a direct answer to each one on your site. Answers get picked up by AI. Slogans
don’t.

**Be specific.** “Quality service you can trust” tells AI nothing. “Licensed techs, same-day furnace
repair, no weekend surcharge” is a sentence it can repeat.

**Make sure AI can read it.** This is the one most people miss. Some
[FAQ widgets and page builders](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) load text using JavaScript,
and AI crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript. The page looks fine to you and shows up blank to
them. Your key answers need to be plain text in the page itself.

**Let AI crawlers in.** Your site has a small file called robots.txt that tells bots what they can
visit. [Some sites block AI crawlers by accident](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt). If they’re
blocked, nothing else on this list matters.

**Keep it current.** Old hours and old prices hurt twice. AI may repeat them, and customers who
notice will trust you less.

## What about SEO?

SEO still matters. It’s the foundation. A site that loads fast, is easy to use, and ranks in regular
search is still worth having, and it helps with everything above.

Getting cited by AI, often called [AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) — answer engine optimization — is the next
floor up. It builds on a good site. It just adds a new question to ask: when an AI answers a question
about my kind of business, can it find me, and does it say my name?
