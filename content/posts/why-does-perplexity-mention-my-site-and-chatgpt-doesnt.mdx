Same question, same business, two different answers. Here’s what’s going on and how to fix it.

<PostTldr>
- Perplexity searches the web every time you ask. ChatGPT often answers from memory, and a small
  business usually isn’t in that memory.
- ChatGPT has its own search crawler. If your site blocks it, even by accident, ChatGPT can’t quote
  you.
- Clear, crawlable answers on your own site give both engines something to pick up.

</PostTldr>

You asked Perplexity about your kind of business, and it named you, with a link to your site. Then
you asked ChatGPT the same question. It named someone else, or it gave a vague list with no names at
all.

It’s frustrating, but it isn’t random. The two tools work differently, and once you see how, most of
the gap makes sense.

## Perplexity looks things up every time

Perplexity is built as a search tool. Each time someone asks a question, it goes out to the web,
reads a handful of pages, and writes an answer with links to its sources.

So if your site has a clear answer to the question, Perplexity has a real shot at finding it that
day. That’s why smaller, local businesses often show up there first.

## ChatGPT doesn’t always look anything up

ChatGPT can search the web, but it doesn’t always. For a lot of questions, it answers from what it
already learned during training. Think of it as answering from memory.

That memory is huge, but it’s weighted toward things that get talked about a lot online: big brands,
national chains, and popular directories. A two-truck gutter company in Columbus probably didn’t
make much of an impression.

When ChatGPT answers from memory, it can only name businesses it remembers. If it doesn’t remember
you, it names someone it does.

## ChatGPT’s search may be locked out of your site

When ChatGPT does search, it uses its own crawler, called OAI-SearchBot. This is separate from
GPTBot, the one that collects pages for training.

Here’s the trap: many websites set up rules a couple of years ago to
[block “the AI bots.”](/blog/should-you-block-ai-crawlers-the-case-for-and-against) Some of those
rules block ChatGPT’s search crawler too. Your site might be wide open to Perplexity and closed to
ChatGPT without you knowing.

It’s not always your robots.txt file, either. Security plugins, firewalls, and hosting settings can
quietly turn bots away as well.

## They trust different sources

Even when both tools search, they don’t pick the same pages. Each one has its own index and its own
sense of which sources to trust. ChatGPT often leans on well-known sites like directories, review
platforms, and big publishers. Perplexity is often happy to quote a specific page from a small
business if it answers the question directly.

So if the main place your business is described well is your own website, Perplexity may find you
and ChatGPT may reach for a directory listing instead.

## Your answers might be hidden

If your FAQ or service details [load with JavaScript](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea),
some crawlers see a nearly blank page. Your visitors see everything. The bots see very little. One
engine might catch it through another route, and another might miss it entirely. Content that’s
written right into the page’s HTML is the safe bet for all of them.

## One more thing: answers change

AI answers aren’t fixed. Ask ChatGPT the same question three times and you may get three slightly
different lists. One test isn’t a verdict. Check a few times, with a few different wordings, before
you decide where you stand.

## How to close the gap

**Keep your SEO in shape.** SEO is still the foundation. Good pages, a sound site, and solid local
listings help every engine find you. [AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) is the next floor up, not a
replacement.

**Let ChatGPT’s search crawler in.** [Check that OAI-SearchBot is allowed](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt)
in your robots.txt, and ask whoever runs your site whether a firewall or security setting is
blocking bots.

**Answer the real questions, clearly.** Put a direct, specific answer at the top: what you do, where,
what it costs, how fast. “Same-week gutter cleaning across Columbus, flat pricing by home size” is
easy to quote. “We’re passionate about service” isn’t.

**Put it in plain HTML on your own site.** No JavaScript required to read it. That way any crawler
that visits gets the full answer, and the credit goes to you.

**Show up in other trusted places.** Keep your business listings and reviews current. They back up
what your site says and give ChatGPT more places to find your name.

**Check again over time.** Getting picked up by ChatGPT
[can take longer](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-ai-search-to-pick-up-your-content) than
Perplexity. Keep checking the questions that matter.
