Most FAQ plugins do the same thing. You install one, fill in your questions, and get a neat
accordion that expands when someone clicks. It looks professional. It probably cost you money. And
to an AI crawler, it is very often a blank space.

## What actually happens when a crawler visits

A browser does two things with a web page: it downloads the HTML, then it runs the JavaScript
that fills in the rest. Most FAQ widgets rely on that second step — the answers aren’t in the page
you download, they’re painted in afterwards by a script.

The crawlers behind AI answers mostly skip that second step. They take the HTML, read what is in
it, and move on. If your answers only exist after JavaScript runs, they were never there to be read.

> You paid for content that is invisible to the exact thing you were trying to reach.

## How to tell, in about thirty seconds

- Open your FAQ page, right-click, and choose “View page source”.
- Press Cmd/Ctrl+F and search for a distinctive phrase from one of your answers.
- If the phrase isn’t there, the answer isn’t in your HTML — and a crawler won’t find it either.

That view-source trick is the honest version of the test, because it shows you the document as it
arrived rather than the page after your browser has finished assembling it. Inspect Element will
happily show you content that a crawler never sees.

## What to do instead

Put the answers in the page. Not in a script, not in an embed that loads from somewhere else, not
inside an iframe — as text, in your own HTML, on your own domain. It is less exciting than a widget
with animations, and it is the entire difference between being quotable and being invisible.
