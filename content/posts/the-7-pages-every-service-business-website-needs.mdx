Every service business website needs seven pages: Home, one page per service, About, Reviews, Service
Area, FAQ, and Contact. Most sites are missing at least two of them.

<PostTldr>
Every service business needs seven pages: Home, one page per service, About, Reviews, Service Area,
FAQ, and Contact.

- Each page answers one kind of question: what you do, where you do it, who you are, whether people
  trust you, and how to reach you.
- AI answer engines get asked those same questions. They cite pages that hold the answer in plain,
  crawlable HTML.

</PostTldr>

A missing page is a missing answer. Customers can’t find it. Google can’t rank it. AI answer engines
like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google’s AI can’t cite it.

Here’s what each page does, and how the same seven pages set you up to get cited by AI.

## Why pages matter

Your website is a set of answers. Each page is where one answer lives.

Someone asks, “Who replaces water heaters in Dover, NH?” That needs a service page and a service area
page. “Are they any good?” That’s your reviews page. “What does it cost?” That’s your FAQ.

No page, no answer. The customer, and the AI, move on to a site that has one.

## 1. Home

Your home page says what you do, where you do it, and how to get in touch. All three, in the first
screen.

Skip the slideshow. Skip “Welcome to our website.” Write the headline like a plain sentence:
“Residential plumbing repair in the Seacoast, NH.” Then your phone number and a button.

## 2. One page per service

This is the page most businesses skip. They list every service on one page and call it done.

Every service you want to be found for needs its own page. Water heater replacement gets a page.
Drain cleaning gets a page. Furnace repair gets a page.

Each service page should cover:

- What the service is, in plain words
- Who it’s for and when you need it
- What the process looks like
- A price range, or what changes the price
- How to book it

One service per page means one clear answer per page. Search engines and answer engines both want
that.

## 3. About

People hire people. Your About page shows who’s behind the business.

Keep it real. How long you’ve been doing this. Who’s on the team. Your licenses and insurance. Why
you started. Real photos of real people beat stock photos every time.

## 4. Reviews

Put your best reviews on your own site, on their own page. Don’t just link to Google.

This is your proof page. When someone asks, “Can I trust this company?” this is the answer. It works
best alongside [a steady flow of new Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) — the page
on your site is the version an AI can read directly.

## 5. Service Area

List the towns you serve by name. Not “the greater metro area.” The actual towns.

If you serve a lot of towns, group them by region. If a few towns are your bread and butter, give
each one a short page of its own. That’s how you show up for “plumber in Portsmouth” and “plumber in
Exeter” instead of neither.

## 6. FAQ

Your FAQ page answers the questions you get on the phone all day. How much does it cost? Do you offer
emergency service? Are you licensed and insured? How fast can you come out?

Write the question the way a customer says it. Answer it in two or three sentences. Don’t dodge
pricing. “Most water heater replacements run $1,800 to $3,200 depending on size and fuel type” is an
answer. “Call for a quote” is not. (Use your own numbers — a price borrowed from an example is a
promise you didn’t make, and an AI will repeat it exactly.)

## 7. Contact

Phone, email, address, hours, and a short form. Keep your phone number in the header of every page,
but still have a Contact page. It’s where people go once they’ve decided. It’s also
[a good place for a short FAQ](/blog/should-you-add-a-faq-on-your-contact-page) — the four or five
questions people ask right before they reach out.

## How these pages set up AEO

SEO is the foundation. These seven pages, with clear headings and plain text, are what get you ranked
in regular search. That hasn’t changed.

[AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) (answer engine optimization) is the next floor up. Answer engines don’t show
a list of links. They give one answer and cite a few sources. To be a source, your site has to hold
the answer in a form the engine can read and trust.

Each page does one part of that job.

**Service pages answer “what.”** Someone asks an AI, “What’s involved in replacing a sump pump?” The
engine looks for a page that explains it plainly. A bulleted list on a generic Services page gives it
nothing to cite. A real sump pump page does.

**Service Area pages answer “where.”** Answer engines get asked local questions all day: “Who does
furnace repair near Hampton, NH?” If the town name isn’t on your site in plain text, you’re not in
the running.

**About and Reviews answer “who” and “can I trust them.”** Licenses, years in business, named team
members, and real reviews on your own domain all help an engine decide you’re safe to recommend.

**The FAQ page answers everything else.** This is the most citable page on any service website. It’s
questions and answers, the same format the engine uses. Ask an assistant “How much does drain
cleaning cost?” and it pulls from pages that state a number.

**Contact answers “how do I reach them.”** Your name, address, and phone should match across every
page and your Google Business Profile. That tells engines you’re one real business.

## Two rules that make it work

**Rule 1: The text has to be in real HTML.** Many website builders load
[FAQ accordions, review widgets, and service lists](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) with
JavaScript after the page opens. AI crawlers don’t run JavaScript. If your answers only show up after
a script runs, the engine sees a blank page. Your answers need to be in the page’s static HTML.

**Rule 2: Answer first, explain second.** Put the direct answer in the first sentence under each
heading. Add detail after. Engines grab the first clear statement they find. Bury it in paragraph
four and they grab it from someone else.

## What to do this week

1. Count your services. Count your service pages. If the numbers don’t match, that’s your first job.
2. Write down the ten questions you answer on the phone most. That’s your FAQ page.
3. Search your site for your town names. If nothing comes up, build the Service Area page.
4. Check your Reviews page. If it’s just a link to Google, bring the reviews onto your site.

You don’t need a redesign. You need the right pages with plain answers on them.
