There isn’t one. There’s a short list that works together — and most electricians are only doing two
of them.

<PostTldr>
The best marketing strategy for an electrician is not a single channel. It’s a small stack: word of
mouth, Google Business Profile, reviews, a simple website, paid ads for urgent jobs, and being
visible to AI answer engines.

- Word of mouth is still the best lead you’ll ever get. Everything else exists to make it happen more
  often and to catch the customers who don’t have someone to ask.
- Start with the free stuff (referrals, reviews, your Google listing), then add one paid channel,
  then make sure AI can read your site. Do them in that order.

</PostTldr>

If you own an electrical business, you’ve probably been pitched every marketing idea under the sun.
Postcards. Radio. A $4,000 website. A guy who “does SEO.” A different guy who swears by Facebook ads.

Here’s the honest answer: there is no single best marketing strategy for electricians. There’s a
handful of things that work, and the businesses that grow do most of them at a basic level rather
than one of them perfectly.

Below is that list, roughly in the order you should tackle it.

## 1. Word of mouth (still number one)

The best customer you’ll get this year will come from someone saying your name out loud. “Call the
guy who did our panel — he showed up on time and didn’t upsell us.” That lead arrives pre-sold.
They’re not comparing you to four other electricians. They just want to know when you can come.

The mistake most electricians make is treating referrals as luck. They’re not. You can make them
happen more often:

- **Ask.** At the end of a job that went well, say, “If you know anyone who needs an electrician,
  I’d appreciate the referral.” Most people never ask.
- **Leave something behind.** A magnet or a card with your number on the panel door. The next
  homeowner will find it too.
- **Reward it.** A small thank-you — a gift card, a discount on the next visit — for customers who
  send you work.
- **Be easy to recommend.** Answer the phone. Show up when you said. Clean up. Boring, but it’s the
  whole game.

Every other strategy on this list is really just a way to get more of this.

## 2. Google Business Profile

When someone types “electrician near me,” the map with three businesses at the top is where most of
the calls go. Getting into that box is free. It’s your Google Business Profile.

Fill it out completely: service area, hours, phone, photos of real work (not stock images), and the
services you actually offer. Post to it now and then. Pick the right primary category (“Electrician,”
not “Contractor”). Keep your name, address, and phone number identical everywhere online.

For a local service business, this one listing usually brings in more calls than the website does.
Treat it like a storefront.

## 3. Reviews

Reviews do two jobs. They help you show up higher on Google, and they close the customer once they’ve
found you. A profile with 8 reviews loses to one with 80, even if you’re the better electrician.

[Make asking part of the job.](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) Text a review link the same day,
while the customer is still happy the lights work. Ask every customer the same way — screening for
the ones you expect to be happy is review gating, and Google actively enforces against it. Reply to
every review, including the bad ones, calmly and briefly. Aim for a steady trickle rather than a
burst.

## 4. A simple website that says the right things

You don’t need a fancy site. You need a fast one that answers the questions people are actually
asking:

- What do you do? (Panel upgrades, EV chargers, generator installs, service calls, and so on.)
- Where do you work? Name the towns.
- Are you licensed and insured? Say so, with the license number.
- How fast can you get here? Do you do emergency calls?
- Roughly what does it cost?

That’s SEO at its most basic: clear pages, one topic per page, your towns and services written in
plain words. If you want the full structure,
[seven pages cover it](/blog/the-7-pages-every-service-business-website-needs). SEO is the foundation
of being found online. Everything below builds on it.

## 5. Paid ads for urgent work

Some electrical jobs can’t wait. A dead panel, a burning smell, a house with no power. Those
customers aren’t reading blog posts. They’re searching and calling the first credible result.

That’s where Google Local Services Ads and search ads earn their keep. You pay per lead or per click,
you only run them for the urgent, high-value jobs, and you turn them off when you’re booked out. Keep
the budget small until you know what a lead costs you and what it’s worth.

Facebook and Instagram ads work less well for urgent jobs but fine for planned ones like EV charger
installs or generators, where a homeowner is thinking about it for weeks.

## 6. Being visible to AI (AEO)

Here’s the newer one, and it’s the one most electricians haven’t heard of yet.

More and more people skip Google entirely and ask an AI instead. “Who’s a good electrician in
Portsmouth for an EV charger?” ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s own AI answers with two or three
names. Whoever gets named gets the call. Nobody clicks a list of ten links.

Making sure you’re one of the names is called [AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo), answer engine optimization.
If SEO is the foundation, AEO is the next floor up. It doesn’t replace your website or your Google
profile. It makes sure the AI can read them.

In practice, that means three things:

- **Answer real questions on your site, in plain text.** “Do you install EV chargers?” “Do you do
  emergency calls on weekends?” “How much does a panel upgrade cost?” A straight answer under each
  one.
- **Make sure the AI can actually see it.** This is the catch. Many
  [FAQ plugins and page builders](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) load answers with
  JavaScript, and AI crawlers mostly don’t run JavaScript. The page looks fine to a person and is
  blank to the AI. Your answers need to be in the plain HTML of your own site.
- **Check whether it’s working.** Ask a few AI tools the questions your customers would ask and see
  whose name comes back. If it’s your competitor’s, you know what to fix.

It’s early, which is the point. Most electricians in your area aren’t doing this yet, so the bar to
get named is low. There’s a
[fuller walkthrough for electricians here](/blog/how-electricians-can-be-found-in-chatgpt).

## 7. Partnerships and the trades network

Realtors, home inspectors, general contractors, HVAC companies, property managers. These people meet
homeowners who need an electrician before the homeowner knows it. A couple of real relationships here
can feed you steady work for years. Buy the coffee. Refer them back.

## 8. Vehicle wrap and yard signs

Old-school, cheap, and it works. A clean wrap on a van that parks in driveways all day is an ad your
neighbours see every week. A yard sign left up for a few days after a job does the same. These won’t
make the phone ring on their own, but they make your name familiar, and familiar names get picked.

## So what’s the actual strategy?

Do them in this order:

1. **Free and fast:** Ask for referrals, finish your Google Business Profile, start collecting
   reviews. A weekend of work.
2. **The foundation:** Get a simple website that names your services and towns and answers the basic
   questions. This is your SEO base.
3. **One paid channel:** Turn on Local Services Ads for urgent jobs. Watch the cost per lead.
4. **The next floor:** Put your answers on your site in a form AI can read, and check whether AI is
   naming you.
5. **Keep it going:** Stay in touch with the trades who refer you, and keep asking for reviews.

None of this is complicated. The electricians who win aren’t doing anything clever. They’re doing the
basics, consistently, and they’re not invisible in the places customers are now asking.
