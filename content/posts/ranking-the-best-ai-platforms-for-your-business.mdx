Not which one you should use. Which ones are already recommending your competitor.

Most “best AI platform” lists answer a question you didn’t ask: which chatbot should you type into.

That’s a fine question if you’re picking a writing assistant. It’s the wrong one if you run a
business. The version that actually costs you money is this: when a customer
[asks an AI which company to call](/blog/why-getting-your-business-mentioned-by-ai-is-a-great-referral),
which platforms are giving the answer — and are you in it?

That’s a ranking you can build from real numbers. So here it is, ordered by how much of your
customer’s decision passes through each one, with the data behind every placement.

## First, an honest note about the data

Two respected trackers can’t agree on the basics. StatCounter had ChatGPT at roughly 77% of
worldwide AI chatbot web visits in June 2026, with Gemini and Perplexity around 8% each. Similarweb
data for May 2026 put ChatGPT at about 54% and Gemini at nearly 28%.

Both are correct. They’re measuring different things — different domains, different devices,
different definitions of “an AI chatbot.” App usage adds anywhere from 15% to 60% on top of web
visits depending on the assistant, and Google’s AI shows up inside a search results page where
nobody counts it as a “chatbot visit” at all.

So treat the order below as directional, not decimal-precise. The gaps that matter here are big
enough to survive the disagreement.

## 1. ChatGPT

The one your customers mean when they say “I asked AI.”

OpenAI last officially confirmed 900 million weekly active users in February 2026, and the app
crossed a billion monthly users in June 2026 — the fastest app in history to get there, per Sensor
Tower data reported by Reuters. By late July, The Information reported weekly actives approaching a
billion too.

But scale isn’t the reason it’s first. Referrals are.
[Conductor put ChatGPT at 87.4%](https://www.conductor.com/academy/aeo-geo-benchmarks-report/) of
average AI referral traffic across industries. eMarketer’s coverage of Grips Intelligence data went higher
still, at 92.2%. Whatever the exact figure, when someone clicks through to a business from an AI
answer, it’s usually ChatGPT that sent them.

The catch: ChatGPT is stingy with citations. It averages fewer sources per answer than its rivals —
roughly 7, versus around 12 for Google AI Overviews — and it leans hard on encyclopedic and
editorial sources. In one analysis of 680 million citations, Wikipedia alone accounted for 47.9% of
its top-10 source share. Fewer slots means each one is worth more, and harder to win.

## 2. Google’s AI surfaces — AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Gemini

Bigger reach than anything else. Just harder to see.

At Google I/O in May 2026, Sundar Pichai put AI Overviews above 2.5 billion monthly users and AI
Mode past 1 billion. The standalone Gemini app reported over 950 million monthly actives at
Alphabet’s Q2 2026 earnings, up from 750 million at the end of 2025.

For a local business, this may genuinely be number one. Whitespark found AI Overviews appearing on
about 68% of local business queries in 2026 — versus 39% for the traditional local pack. And Ahrefs’
analysis of 300,000 keywords found that when an AI Overview appears, clicks to the #1 organic result
drop by 58%.

Read that twice. Ranking first is worth substantially less than it was, on the exact queries where
local businesses live.

The catch: Google’s AI reach is enormous but the traffic is nearly invisible in your analytics,
because it’s folded into ordinary Search reporting. Big influence, faint footprint.

## 3. Perplexity

Small audience. Loud citations.

Perplexity is nowhere near the top on users — figures range from around 45 million monthly actives
on the core product to 100 million-plus across everything it now sells. On web visit share it sits
in the low single digits.

It punches far above that for one reason: it cites more than anyone. Depending on the dataset,
Perplexity shows somewhere between 16 and 22 sources per answer, roughly triple ChatGPT. In one
34,000-response study, Perplexity mentioned brands 13.05% of the time while ChatGPT managed 0.59%.

The catch: its source diet is unusual. Reddit alone made up 46.7% of Perplexity’s top-10 citation
share in Profound’s data. Winning here has as much to do with being discussed by real people as with
what’s on your own site.

## 4. Microsoft Copilot

Low consumer share, serious workplace distribution.

Copilot barely registers on public chatbot rankings — around 1–4% depending on the tracker. But it
reaches roughly 420 million monthly users through Microsoft 365, with about 20 million paid seats as
of Q3 FY2026. That’s a lot of people asking questions from inside Word and Outlook, on the clock.

There’s an eye-catching data point here: Microsoft Clarity studied 1,277 domains and found Copilot
referrals converting at 17 times the rate of direct traffic. Tiny volume, unusually serious intent.

Priority: high if you sell to businesses. Low if you’re a bakery.

## 5. Claude

The one moving fastest in B2B referrals.

Claude sits in the low-to-mid single digits on most consumer trackers, but it’s the only line
trending decisively upward in business referral data. One tracking wave measured its share of B2B AI
referrals rising from 11.8% to 18.5% between waves, with a single-month reading of 27.2% in April
2026.

It’s also unusually generous to brands’ own websites. Slate HQ tracked 300,000+ citations across six
B2B brands and found Claude gave the highest share of citations to a brand’s own content, at 9.1% —
ahead of Perplexity at 6.8%, with ChatGPT consistently the least brand-friendly.

Priority: worth watching if you sell software, services, or anything with a professional buyer.

## 6. Grok and Meta AI — not yet

Meta AI reported 1.2 billion monthly users across Meta’s apps in Q1 2026, which sounds like it
belongs at the top. It doesn’t, for your purposes: those are largely people bumping into an
assistant inside Instagram and WhatsApp, not asking for a contractor recommendation and clicking out
to a website. Grok sits around 2.4% of web share and is heavily bundled with X Premium.

Neither is a meaningful referral source for a small business today. Revisit in a year.

## The finding that should change your plan

Here’s the part that surprises people, and it matters more than the order above.

These platforms barely overlap. A study of 161,286 prompts in May–June 2026 found the major engines
shared only about 17% of their cited sources for the same question — and just 3.8% of sources were
cited by all of them. Separate analysis of 680 million citations found only 11% domain overlap
between ChatGPT and Perplexity.

So “am I visible in AI?” isn’t one question with one answer. You can be the top recommendation in
Perplexity and completely absent from ChatGPT, for the same query, on the same day. Checking one
engine and assuming the rest look similar is like checking your Google ranking and assuming it tells
you about Bing.

## What works on every one of them

The platforms diverge. The fundamentals don’t.

People are already doing this. BrightLocal’s 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 45% had used an AI
tool to find a local business in the past year — up from 6% the year before. That makes AI the third
most-used local discovery channel, behind only Google and Facebook, ahead of Yelp and Tripadvisor.
In the same window, Google’s share of review discovery fell from 83% to 71%.

And the traffic is good traffic. Adobe Digital Insights found AI-referred visitors to US retail
sites converting 42% better than non-AI traffic in March 2026 — a channel that had converted 38%
worse twelve months earlier. Those visitors also spent 48% more time on site and generated 37% more
revenue per visit.

But almost nobody is set up for it. One estimate puts 88% of local businesses with no active
strategy for appearing in AI results. In one measurement, ChatGPT surfaced just 1.2% of all local
business locations.

Four things move the needle regardless of platform:

**Answer the questions people actually ask.** In plain language, the way a customer would type it,
with a direct answer in the first sentence. Answers get quoted. Slogans don’t.

**Be specific.** “Committed to quality” is unquotable. “Licensed techs, same-day AC repair in Tampa,
no after-hours surcharge” is a sentence an AI can lift straight into an answer.

**Make it crawlable.** This is the one that quietly sinks businesses. If your FAQ is rendered by a
[JavaScript widget](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea), AI crawlers generally don’t run
JavaScript — so it looks perfect to visitors and doesn’t exist to the engines. It has to be real
HTML on your own domain, present the moment the page loads.

**Mind the off-page half.** One analysis of 23,000+ citations found roughly 77% of the sources cited
in AI answers about a brand were off-page — directories, reviews, forums, video. Your reviews and
your listings aren’t separate from AI visibility; they’re a large part of it.

One honest caveat while we’re here:
[Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages adding schema](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/) in 2026 and
found JSON-LD [schema](/seo-guide) produced no measurable change in AI citations across AI Overviews,
AI Mode and ChatGPT — its own summary is that “adding schema produced no major uplift in citations on
any platform.” Schema is worth having for
machine-readability and for search generally — but the plain, crawlable text is what does the actual
work. Don’t let anyone sell you schema as the magic.

And remember the last step: Yext’s 2026 survey found that although about three-quarters of AI users
rate their trust in AI local recommendations at 4 or 5 out of 5, only 5% go straight from AI answer
to purchase. Most verify first — 53% search Google, 49% go to the business’s website. The AI hands
them your name. Your site still has to close it.
