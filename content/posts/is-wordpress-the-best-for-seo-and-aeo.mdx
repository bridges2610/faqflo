Short answer: WordPress is one of the best platforms for SEO and AEO, but it won’t do the work for
you. A well-set-up Squarespace site will beat a messy WordPress site every time.

Here’s why, and what to check on your own site.

## Why people say WordPress is the best

WordPress runs about 4 in 10 websites on the internet. That’s a lot of trust, and there are good
reasons for it.

**You control almost everything.** Page titles, page addresses, your sitemap, your robots.txt file
(the note that tells search engines and AI what they’re allowed to read). If you want to change it,
you can.

**Pages load as real HTML.** When someone — or something — visits a WordPress page, the server sends
the finished page. The words are already there. That matters a lot for AI, and we’ll get to why in a
second.

**There’s a tool for everything.** SEO plugins help you write good titles, build a sitemap, and add
schema (hidden labels that tell search engines what your content means).

**Publishing is easy.** Writing a new page or blog post takes minutes, and anyone on your team can
do it. That matters, because the sites that get found are the ones that keep adding clear answers.

**Your content is yours.** You can move it, copy it, or change hosts whenever you want.

So yes — WordPress gives you a great foundation. But a foundation isn’t a house.

## SEO first, then AEO

Think of it like a building.

**SEO is the foundation.** It helps you show up when someone searches Google and scrolls a list of
links. Fast pages, clear titles, good content, other sites linking to you. You still need all of it.

**AEO is the next floor up.** [AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) stands for Answer Engine Optimization. It’s
about getting your business named when someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI Overviews a
question — like “Who does same-day AC repair near me?”

Good SEO helps AEO. But AEO adds a few new rules, and that’s where platforms like WordPress can trip
you up.

## Where WordPress goes wrong for AI

Most AI crawlers read the raw HTML of your page and leave. They generally don’t run JavaScript, the
code that makes pages move and change after they load.

WordPress sends real HTML, so you start in good shape. The problem is what you add on top.

**FAQ plugins.** [Many FAQ and accordion plugins](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) load
their answers with JavaScript. Your visitors see a nice list of questions. The AI sees nothing. Your
best answers — the exact thing an AI would love to quote — are invisible.

**Security settings.** Some security plugins, firewalls, and hosting companies now block AI crawlers
by default. Your site could be turning away ChatGPT and Perplexity without you ever clicking a
button.

**Plugin pile-up.** Every plugin adds weight. Too many, and your pages get slow. Slow pages hurt SEO,
and a site that times out can get skipped by crawlers.

**Heavy page builders.** Some drag-and-drop builders stack up extra code. Usually the words still
come through, but it’s worth checking.

None of this is WordPress’s fault. It’s just that WordPress lets you add almost anything — including
things that hurt you.

## What about Squarespace, Wix, Webflow, and Shopify?

Here’s the honest version.

**Squarespace** sends real HTML and handles the basics well. You get less control than WordPress,
but less to break.

**Webflow** gives you clean code and lots of control. It’s a favorite of designers for a reason.

**Shopify** is built for stores and handles product pages well. Its blog and page tools are simpler,
but they work.

**Wix** has improved a lot for SEO. The thing to watch: some site builders place pasted HTML inside
a frame (a little window from another address). Content in a frame may not count as part of your
page. Always check that pasted content shows up in your page’s real source.

The pattern: any of these can work. The question isn’t “which platform?” It’s “can AI read my
answers, and are they good answers?”

## How to check your site in 5 minutes

You don’t need to switch platforms. You need to check a few things.

1. **Look at your page source.** Open your FAQ page, right-click, and choose “View Page Source.”
   Press Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F) and search for one of your answers. If you can find it, AI can too. If you
   can’t, it’s hidden behind JavaScript.
2. **Check your robots.txt.** Type your website address followed by /robots.txt. Look for lines that
   block GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or Google-Extended. If they’re blocked,
   [AI can’t read your site](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt).
3. **Read your answers out loud.** Does the first sentence actually answer the question? “We’re
   passionate about quality” isn’t an answer. “We fix AC units same-day in Tampa with no after-hours
   fee” is.
4. **Check your speed.** If your pages feel slow on your phone, they’re slow for crawlers too. Remove
   plugins you don’t use.
