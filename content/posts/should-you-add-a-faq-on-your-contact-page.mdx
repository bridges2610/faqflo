## Why the contact page is the wrong place to leave people guessing

Think about what someone is doing on your contact page. They’ve already decided you might be the
right fit. They’re one step from calling or sending a message.

Then a small question stops them. Are they even open on Saturdays? Do they come out to my town? Am I
going to get a reply today or next week?

If the answer isn’t on the page, some people will guess. A lot of people will leave. Not because you
couldn’t help them — because they didn’t know for sure.

A short FAQ right there answers the question before it becomes a reason to bounce. That’s the whole
case for it.

## What belongs on a contact page FAQ

Not your full FAQ. That
[lives on its own page](/blog/what-questions-should-be-on-your-faq-page), and it should. The contact
page FAQ is smaller and more specific. It only covers the questions people ask right before they get
in touch.

For most businesses that’s some mix of:

- **Hours.** When are you open, and when do you answer messages?
- **Service area.** Which towns, zip codes, or regions do you cover?
- **Response time.** “We reply to every message within one business day.” One sentence, and it
  removes a lot of doubt.
- **The best way to reach you.** Call for emergencies, form for quotes, email for billing — whatever
  is true.
- **Pricing basics.** Do you charge for estimates? Is there a minimum? You don’t need a price list.
  You need to answer the question people are nervous to ask.
- **What happens next.** “We’ll call to confirm a time, then send a written quote.” People like
  knowing the steps.

Four to six questions is plenty. If you’re past eight, you’re rebuilding your main FAQ page and
burying the form.

## Where to put it

Below the form or contact details. Never above them.

The contact page has one job: make it easy to reach you. The FAQ supports that job. It shouldn’t
compete with it. Someone who already knows they want to call shouldn’t have to scroll past six
questions to find the number.

A good layout looks like this: phone, email, and form at the top. Address and hours next. Then a
heading like “Before you reach out” with your short FAQ underneath.

## The part most people miss: AI reads your contact page

Here’s the reason this matters more in 2026 than it did a few years ago.

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI something like “Is there a plumber in Concord
who does weekend calls?” the AI goes looking for facts. Hours. Service area. Whether you do
emergencies. Those facts are exactly what a contact page FAQ contains.

If those answers are written in plain text on your site, the AI can find them, quote them, and name
your business. If they’re missing — or trapped inside a JavaScript widget the AI can’t read — it
names someone else.

This is the layer above regular SEO. SEO is still the foundation: a clear, well-built contact page
that Google can index and rank. [AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) (answer engine optimization) is the next
floor up — writing your answers so an AI can lift them straight into a recommendation. A contact page
FAQ does both jobs at once if you build it right.

## How to build it right

Three rules.

**Answer first, then explain.** “Yes, we serve all of Rockingham County. That includes Portsmouth,
Exeter, Hampton, and Dover.” The first sentence is the answer. The rest is detail. That’s the shape
an AI can quote and a hurried person can skim.

**Be specific.** “We respond fast” says nothing. “We reply within one business day, usually the same
afternoon” is a fact someone can plan around — and a fact an AI can repeat.

**Use real HTML, not a plugin.** Most
[FAQ plugins and accordion widgets](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) load their answers with
JavaScript. Visitors see them. AI crawlers usually don’t, because they read the raw page and don’t
run scripts. Your answers need to be in the page itself, on your own domain. A plain heading and
paragraph for each question works. So does a copy-paste HTML block from a tool built for this.

## Two worked examples

Here’s what the three rules look like in practice. Five questions each, in the order they’d appear
under the form.

⚠️ These are templates, not facts. **Swap in your own numbers before you publish anything.** A price,
a response time or a service area copied from an example is a promise you didn’t make — and the whole
point of this exercise is that an AI will repeat it word for word.

### A dental practice

**Are you accepting new patients?**
Yes, we’re accepting new patients for general and family dentistry. New patient appointments are
usually available within two weeks.

**Do you take my insurance?**
We’re in-network with Delta Dental, Cigna, and MetLife, and we file claims for most other PPO plans
as an out-of-network provider. Call us with your plan name and we’ll check before you book.

**Can I be seen today if I’m in pain?**
Yes. We hold same-day emergency slots every weekday and see most urgent cases within two hours. Call
the office rather than using the form — the phone is monitored all day.

**What does a first visit cost without insurance?**
A new patient exam, cleaning and X-rays is $189. If treatment is needed, you’ll get a written plan
with costs before anything is scheduled.

**What happens after I book?**
You’ll get a confirmation text and a short medical history form to fill in beforehand. Arrive ten
minutes early for your first visit and we’ll take it from there.

### A roofing company

**Do you cover my town?**
We cover all of Rockingham County, including Portsmouth, Exeter, Hampton, Dover and Stratham. If
you’re just outside that, call anyway and we’ll tell you honestly whether the trip makes sense.

**Do you charge for estimates?**
No. Roof inspections and written estimates are free, and there’s no obligation. We’ll send the quote
in writing the same day we visit.

**How fast can you get out for a leak?**
Same day for active leaks in our service area. Emergency tarping is a flat $450, credited toward the
repair if you go ahead with us.

**Do you handle insurance claims?**
Yes. We document the damage, meet your adjuster on site, and bill the claim directly. You deal with
us, not with the paperwork.

**What happens after I request a quote?**
We’ll call to confirm a time, inspect the roof and take photos, then email a written quote with
options and prices. No one will show up unannounced.

Read those out loud. Every one of them leads with the answer, names a real place or number, and would
fit inside an AI’s reply without editing. That’s the standard.

## When you might skip it

There are a couple of cases where a contact page FAQ isn’t worth it.

If your contact page is just a phone number and an address with almost no traffic, start by fixing
your main FAQ page and your Google Business Profile first. Come back to this later.

And if every question you’d list is already answered clearly in the two lines above the form — hours,
area, response time, right there — you may not need a separate FAQ section. The point is that the
answers exist on the page. The format is secondary.

For everyone else, it’s one of the cheapest improvements you can make to a page that already gets
your most motivated visitors.
