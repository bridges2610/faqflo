## Why this matters for plumbers specifically

Picture it. It’s 9pm on a Sunday. Water is coming through the kitchen ceiling. The homeowner grabs
their phone and types into ChatGPT: “Who does emergency plumbing in Manchester NH tonight?”

ChatGPT names two companies. Maybe three. It says a sentence about each — “licensed, 24/7, no
after-hours surcharge.” The homeowner calls the first one.

That’s the whole sales cycle. No search results page, no scrolling, no comparing five websites. One
question, a short list, a phone call.

Plumbing is one of the best-fit trades for this shift because the need is urgent and the question is
simple. People aren’t researching. They want a name they can trust, right now. If that name is yours,
you win the job before you pick up the phone. If it’s your competitor’s, you never knew the call
existed.

## How ChatGPT decides who to recommend

This is the part that surprises most owners: ChatGPT isn’t pulling from a directory you can pay into.
It’s reading the open web — your site, your Google Business Profile, your reviews, local listings —
and building an answer from whatever it can find and understand.

So it comes down to two questions:

- **Can it read you?** If your details are trapped somewhere a crawler can’t reach, you don’t exist
  to it.
- **Can it quote you?** If it can read you but everything says “quality service you can trust,”
  there’s nothing specific to repeat.

Everything below is about answering yes to both.

## Step 1: Make sure AI can actually see your website

Your regular SEO — a fast site, a good Google Business Profile, solid reviews — is still the
foundation. [AI search is the next floor up](/blog/what-is-aeo), and it needs a couple of things the
foundation doesn’t cover.

**Check your robots.txt.** This is a small file on your site that tells crawlers what they’re allowed
to look at. [Many hosts and security plugins block AI crawlers by default](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt)
— names like GPTBot (ChatGPT), ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and Google-Extended. If they’re blocked,
you’ve locked the front door on the exact visitors you want. Ask whoever built your site to allow
them, or check it yourself at `yoursite.com/robots.txt`.

**Get your important text out of JavaScript.** AI crawlers read the raw HTML of your page. They
generally don’t run JavaScript the way a browser does. So if your services, hours, and FAQs load in
after the page appears — which is how most
[FAQ plugins and accordion widgets](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) work — the crawler sees
a blank spot. Your visitors see a nice dropdown. The AI sees nothing.

A quick test: right-click your page, choose “View page source,” and search for a sentence from your
FAQ. If it isn’t there, AI can’t read it either.

## Step 2: Answer the questions people actually ask

ChatGPT builds recommendations out of answers. Give it the answers.

Think about what people type when they’re looking for a plumber — not marketing themes, the real
questions:

- Do you do emergency calls on nights and weekends?
- How much does it cost to replace a water heater?
- Do you cover my town?
- Do you charge a trip fee?
- Are you licensed and insured?
- How fast can you get here?

Every one of those deserves a direct, two- or three-sentence answer on your website, in plain text.
Not a “Contact us to learn more.” A real answer.

This is why a FAQ page is such a strong tool for plumbers. It’s already shaped like the questions
people ask an AI. It just has to be written honestly and published somewhere a crawler can read it.

## Step 3: Be specific enough to quote

An AI can’t repeat a slogan. It repeats facts.

Compare these two:

> “We’re a family-owned plumbing company committed to quality and customer satisfaction.”

> “Licensed plumbers serving Manchester, Nashua, and Concord. Same-day service, 24/7 emergency calls,
> and no after-hours surcharge. Water heater replacement starts at $1,400 installed.”

The first one is unquotable. There’s nothing in it that answers a question. The second one is the
ChatGPT answer — an AI can lift that sentence straight into its recommendation, and it’s the sentence
that gets the homeowner to call. (Use your own numbers, of course. A price borrowed from an example
is a promise you didn’t make, and an AI will repeat it word for word.)

Get specific about:

- **Service area** — name the towns. “Greater Manchester” is vague. “Manchester, Bedford, Goffstown,
  Hooksett” is a match when someone asks about Hooksett.
- **Hours and emergency coverage** — say exactly when you answer the phone.
- **Pricing** — at least starting prices or a trip-fee number. Owners hate publishing prices. AI
  loves quoting them, and so do customers.
- **Credentials** — license number, insurance, years in business.
- **The jobs you actually do** — water heaters, sump pumps, frozen pipes, drain cleaning, gas lines.
  List them.

None of this is gaming anything. You’re briefing a referral source that never sleeps, the same way
you’d make sure your best customer knows your service area before she recommends you.

## Step 4: Publish it as plain HTML on your own site

Where the answers live matters as much as what they say.

The content has to be real, readable HTML on your own domain — the moment the page loads, no
JavaScript needed to reveal it. That’s what a crawler can read, trust, and cite, with the credit
pointing back to you.

If your site is on WordPress, paste it into a Custom HTML block. Squarespace, a code block. Webflow,
an embed. Most builders have a spot for it. The one thing to avoid is a plugin that hides your
answers behind a click, because that’s the version the AI never sees.

## Step 5: Check whether it’s working

Once your answers are live, ask the question yourself. Open ChatGPT and type what a customer would
type: “best plumber in [your town],” “emergency plumber near [your town] on Sunday.” See whose names
come back.

Do this for a handful of questions, and check again every few weeks. Answers shift as your
competitors update their sites and as the AI models change. If a competitor is being named for a
question you can answer better, that’s your next FAQ to write.

Being eligible to be cited — crawlable, specific, and answering the real questions — is the part you
control. Actually getting named is the upside you earn on top of it.
