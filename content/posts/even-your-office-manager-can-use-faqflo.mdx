Here’s a question worth asking: who in your business actually knows what customers ask?

It’s probably not you. It’s the person at the front desk. The one who picks up the phone forty times
a day and hears the same six questions over and over. Do you take my insurance. How fast can someone
get here. Do you work weekends. What does it cost.

That person is the best AEO asset you have, and they don’t know it yet.

## The job is smaller than it sounds

When people hear “get your business showing up in AI answers,” they picture a project. Meetings. A
consultant. Something with a login you have to keep track of.

It isn’t that. The whole job is three steps, and your office manager can do all three in an
afternoon.

**One: pick the questions.** FaqFlo shows you the questions people actually ask AI about businesses
like yours. Your office manager reads the list and thinks, yes, people ask me that constantly. They
add the ones we missed. Nobody needs to know what a keyword is.

**Two: click generate.** FaqFlo writes the answers. Direct first sentence, specific facts, no fluff —
the way answer engines like to quote things.

**Three: paste it on the site.** You get a block of HTML. It goes into your website where you’d paste
anything else — the custom HTML block in WordPress, the code block in Squarespace, the embed in
Webflow. Copy, paste, save.

That’s it. That’s the job.

## “But they’re not technical”

They don’t need to be.

There’s no plugin to install. No account to connect. No API key, no DNS record, no developer waiting
on a ticket. The only technical-sounding moment in the entire process is pasting a block of text into
a box, and if your office manager has ever added a Google Map or a booking widget to your site,
they’ve already done something harder.

The reason this works is that FaqFlo does the complicated part invisibly. Underneath, it’s checking
whether AI crawlers can read your site at all, structuring the answers so they can be quoted, and
generating the schema that tells engines what your content means. That’s real work. It just isn’t
work anybody has to look at.

## Why it has to be pasted at all

This is the one piece worth understanding, and it takes ten seconds.

Most FAQ tools build their answers with JavaScript — the code that makes the little accordion expand
when you tap it. A person’s browser runs that code, so the answers show up fine for visitors.

AI crawlers usually don’t run JavaScript. They read the raw page and move on. So
[a FAQ plugin](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) looks perfect to your customers and is
completely invisible to ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini.

Pasting real HTML onto your own site is what fixes that. The answers are in the page from the first
second, on your domain, where the credit and the click come back to you. It’s a slightly less magical
step than “install our plugin,” and it’s the only version that actually works.
