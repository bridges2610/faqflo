Someone asks ChatGPT, “Who does emergency furnace repair near me?”

The answer comes back with three business names and a sentence about each. Underneath, or off to
the side, there are a few little source links.

Those are two separate things. The names in the answer are **mentions**. The links underneath are
**citations**. They don’t always point at the same businesses, and that gap is where most small
businesses are quietly losing.

## Being named is the recommendation

When an AI says your business name in an answer, you got the referral. The customer now knows you
exist, and they know why you were suggested — “open on weekends”, “flat pricing”, “licensed and
insured”.

That’s the moment that turns into a phone call. They may never click anything. They’ll type your
name into Google Maps, or tap the number, or just remember you.

If you only get to win one of these two, win this one. Being named is the sale.

## Being cited is the credit

When an AI cites you, it’s showing its work. It’s saying: here’s where this came from, and it came
from your website.

A citation does three things a mention doesn’t.

**It can send you a click.** Some people do tap the source link. That’s real traffic that never
shows up if you’re only mentioned.

**It’s proof the engine can read your site.** A citation means a crawler reached your page,
understood it, and trusted it enough to point at it. That’s a health check you can’t fake.

**It makes the mention stickier.** When the engine has your own page to pull from, the details it
repeats are your details — your hours, your service area, your prices. Not a guess, and not a
five-year-old directory listing.

## The four places you can be standing

Here’s the useful part. Every business is in one of four spots, and each one means something
different.

The bottom-right box is the honest starting point for most small businesses, and it’s almost always
the same cause: there’s nothing about the business the crawlers can actually read.

## Why the two don’t move together

Naming and citing come from different places, which is why fixing one doesn’t automatically fix the
other.

**Naming draws on everything the AI knows about you.** Its training data, review sites,
directories, local listings, news, forums, and whatever it pulled up in the moment.
[Your website is one input among many](/blog/what-else-influences-companies-being-mentioned-in-ai).
You influence it, but you don’t own it.

**Citing is live.** The engine went and fetched pages while answering, then linked what it used.
That’s a decision made in seconds, based on what’s sitting in your page’s HTML right now.

That difference is why citation is the lever worth pulling first. You can’t rewrite what the
internet already says about you this afternoon. You can change what’s on your own site, and that’s
exactly what the citing half of the system reads.

## The mistake almost nobody catches

Most businesses that check on this at all check one thing: *“Did it say my name?”*

So they ask ChatGPT a question, see their name, and decide they’re fine.

Then they miss the second half. If every source link under that answer points to a directory, the AI
is describing your business using somebody else’s summary of it. When your prices change, or you add
a service, or you expand your service area, the answer doesn’t update — because the page it’s
reading isn’t yours and nobody’s updating it for you.

You’re being recommended from a snapshot. That’s fine right up until the snapshot is wrong, and by
then you have no way to correct it.

The reverse mistake is just as common: watching only for traffic from AI tools. Most AI answers are
zero-click. If clicks are your only measure, you’ll conclude AI isn’t sending you anything while it
quietly recommends you all week.

## What to do about each gap

**If you’re named but not cited:** you need crawlable content on your own domain that answers the
same questions the directories are answering for you. Real HTML, present the moment the page loads.
Most [FAQ plugins render answers with JavaScript](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea), and AI
crawlers generally don’t run JavaScript — so those answers might as well not exist.

**If you’re cited but not named:** your content is readable but generic. Add the specifics that make
you a recommendable business rather than a helpful article. Names, towns, response times, prices,
credentials. “Same-day service in Concord and Manchester, licensed and insured, no after-hours
surcharge” is a sentence an AI can lift straight into a recommendation. “We’re committed to quality”
is not.

**If you’re neither:** start with whether AI can read your site at all.
[That check takes about ten seconds](/free-report), and for a lot of small businesses it’s the
entire problem.
