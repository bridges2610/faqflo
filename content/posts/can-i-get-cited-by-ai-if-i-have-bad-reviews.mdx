## The short answer

You can get cited by AI with bad reviews. Being cited comes down to one thing: can the AI find
clear, specific, readable content about your business? If it can, you’re in the running. If it
can’t, a 5-star rating won’t save you.

That said, “cited” and “recommended” are not the same thing. Understanding the difference is the
whole game here.

## Cited vs. recommended

When someone asks an AI a question, it pulls an answer together from whatever it can read about
you. There are two kinds of questions, and they treat reviews very differently.

**Factual questions.** “Do you do emergency calls on weekends?” “How much is a basic cleaning?” “Do
you cover Dover?” The AI wants a fact. The best source for a fact about your business is your
business. If your site answers the question in plain text, you get cited — reviews barely come into
it. Nobody’s Google rating changes their opening hours.

**Judgment questions.** “Who’s the best plumber in town?” “Is this dentist any good?” Now the AI is
being asked for an opinion, and it borrows one.
[Reviews, review counts, and how you show up on other sites](/blog/does-ai-use-my-google-reviews-how-reviews-affect-ai-recommendations)
all feed into that. A long run of 1-star reviews makes it less likely you’re named for a “best”
question, and if you are named, the AI may repeat the complaint out loud.

Most of the questions customers ask are the first kind. That’s good news. It means the biggest lever
is still content you control.

## What bad reviews actually change

Three things, and none of them is “you’re banned from AI answers.”

**What gets said about you.** If the AI can read 40 reviews saying you were late and nothing from
you explaining what changed, “some customers report late arrivals” ends up in the answer. Silence
lets the reviews write your description.

**Whether you’re the pick for “best.”** For head-to-head recommendation questions, a competitor with
better reviews and equally readable content will usually win. You can still be cited as an option.
You’re less likely to be cited as _the_ option.

**What happens after the citation.** An AI mention is a referral, but it’s a thin one. Most people
do a quick check before they call — and that check is usually your reviews. Getting cited gets you
the look. Reviews decide whether the look turns into a call.

## What to do about it

You can’t delete bad reviews, but you can change what an AI reads next to them.

**Fix the thing.** If the reviews are about one recurring problem, fix that first. Everything else
is a coat of paint.

**Reply to every review, especially the bad ones.** A calm, specific reply is content. It shows the
AI (and the customer) that the problem was handled. “We were understaffed in March and it showed.
We’ve added two techs and now guarantee a 2-hour window” is a sentence an AI can quote.

**Get fresh reviews.** Recent reviews matter more than old ones.
[Ask happy customers at the moment they’re happiest](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) — right
after the job, in person. Ten recent 5-stars change the story faster than arguing with one old
1-star.

**Answer the complaint on your own site.** This is the part almost nobody does. If people complain
about pricing surprises, add an FAQ: “Will there be hidden fees?” with a plain, specific answer. Now
when someone asks the AI about your pricing, the answer comes from you, not from a review.

**Make sure the AI can read it.** None of this helps if your FAQ is trapped in a
[JavaScript widget](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea). AI crawlers generally don’t run
JavaScript, so those answers are invisible to them. Your answers need to be real, crawlable HTML on
your own domain.

## Where SEO fits

Reviews have always been part of SEO — they’re a big piece of how you rank in the local map results,
and that hasn’t changed. Keep doing that work. It’s the foundation. [AEO](/blog/what-is-aeo) is the
next floor up: it takes the same readable, specific content and makes sure it’s shaped so an AI can
quote it. Good reviews help both. Readable answers help both. Neither replaces the other.
