A homeowner in your town has a breaker that keeps tripping. It’s 9pm. They don’t Google it anymore.
They open ChatGPT and type, “Who’s a good electrician in [town] that does emergency calls?”

A few seconds later they get an answer. Two names, maybe three, with a sentence about each. They pick
one and send a text.

That’s the whole job market for electricians, happening quietly in a chat window. And right now, for
most electrical contractors, ChatGPT has nothing to say about them at all.

## Why ChatGPT doesn’t know you exist

ChatGPT isn’t a phone book. It doesn’t have a list of every licensed electrician in your county. When
someone asks it for a recommendation, it goes looking for content it can read about businesses like
yours — your website, your Google profile, review sites, anywhere your details are written down as
plain text.

If it finds clear, specific information, it can confidently name you. If it finds nothing useful, it
doesn’t say “I’m not sure.” It names the electrician down the road whose site it could read.

Here’s the part most electricians never hear: the way a lot of contractor websites are built hides
the good stuff from AI. Your FAQ is in an
[accordion widget](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea). Your services are in a slider. Your
service area is in an image. All of that looks fine to a person with a browser. To an AI crawler —
which reads the raw page and generally doesn’t run JavaScript — it’s blank.

So the question isn’t “Is my website nice?” It’s “If a machine read my website with the pictures and
animations turned off, would it know what I do, where I do it, and what it costs?”

## SEO still matters. This is the next floor up.

None of this replaces the basics. A working website, a Google Business Profile, and
[real reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) are the foundation. Keep doing that.

But ranking on a search results page and being named inside an AI answer are two different things.
The first gets you on a list of ten. The second gets you on a list of two. Getting found in ChatGPT
is [a layer on top of SEO](/blog/what-is-aeo), not a replacement for it. The good news is that it’s
mostly about being clear, not about being clever.

## What ChatGPT actually wants from an electrician

When an AI writes a recommendation, it’s pulling sentences out of whatever it can read. Your job is
to give it sentences worth pulling. Four things matter.

**1. Answer the questions people actually ask.** Not “Our mission is excellence in electrical
service.” Real questions:

- Do you do emergency calls on nights and weekends?
- How much does a panel upgrade cost?
- Are you licensed and insured?
- Do you install EV chargers?
- What towns do you cover?
- Can you fix a breaker that keeps tripping?

Each one gets a direct answer, in plain words, in one or two sentences. That’s exactly the shape
ChatGPT likes to quote.

**2. Be specific.** “We’re committed to quality” is unquotable. “Licensed master electrician,
same-day service in Portsmouth and Dover, $95 diagnostic fee applied to the repair” is a sentence an
AI can lift straight into an answer — and it’s the sentence that closes the customer. (Use your own
numbers, obviously. A price copied from an example is a promise you didn’t make, and an AI will
repeat it word for word.)

Name your towns. Name your services. Give a starting price where you can. Say how fast you can get
there. Specific beats polished every time.

**3. Put it where the machines can read it.** This is the one that sinks most contractors. Your
answers need to live as real, crawlable text on your own website — in the page’s HTML the moment it
loads, no JavaScript required to reveal it. Not in an accordion plugin. Not in a PDF. Not on a page
that only you know about.

And it has to be your domain. When the AI cites the answer, the credit and the click should come to
you, not to some directory.

**4. Check whether it’s working.** Open ChatGPT and ask it the questions your customers ask. “Who
does emergency electrical work in [town]?” “Best electrician for an EV charger install near [town]?”
See whose names come back. If it isn’t you, you now know exactly what to fix. Do this every month or
so — answers shift as competitors update their sites.

## The 20-minute version

If you have a website and twenty minutes, here’s the order:

1. Write down the ten questions customers ask you on the phone every week.
2. Answer each one in two sentences. Include towns, services, and prices where you can.
3. Paste those answers onto your site as plain text — a “Questions” page or a section on your
   homepage. Make sure it’s not buried in a widget.
4. Ask ChatGPT one of those questions and see if you show up.

That’s it. Most electricians who do this are ahead of nearly every competitor in their area, because
nearly nobody is doing it yet.
