## There are three clocks, not one

“How long until AI picks up my content” is really three questions, and they run on very different
schedules.

**Crawled.** A bot fetched your page. This is the fast one — often the same day you publish.

**Indexed.** Your page is now in the pool the engine can pull from when someone asks a question.
Days to a couple of weeks.

**Cited.** An AI actually names you in an answer. Weeks — and it isn’t automatic just because the
first two happened.

Most of the confusion comes from mixing up the second and third. Being findable and being chosen
are different things.

## Engine by engine

**Perplexity is the fastest.** It runs a background index and a live web search on every query, so
freshly published content can turn up in its answers almost immediately.

**ChatGPT is fast — when it searches at all.** OpenAI’s `OAI-SearchBot` builds ChatGPT’s search
index, and it visits most sites every few days or weeks rather than several times a day. There’s a
bigger catch: a 2026 analysis found ChatGPT only runs a live web search on about a third of
prompts — the rest are answered from what the model already knows. So a brand-new page can only win
the searched share.

**Google AI Overviews runs on Google’s clock.** If Google has indexed the page, AI Overviews can
cite it — which means the timeline is simply your normal Google indexing timeline. For an
established site that’s days. For a brand-new small-business site, it can be weeks, and sometimes
it just doesn’t happen.

**Copilot, Grok and Claude mostly ride the same rails.** Bing’s index feeds several of them, which
is why getting into Bing matters more than most small businesses realise.

One published test makes the speed difference concrete: a new page was crawled by ChatGPT and
Perplexity on the day it went live, while Bing and Google didn’t come by until one to two days
later. AI crawlers are not the slow part.

## What actually decides your timeline

Six dependencies, roughly in order of how much they matter.

1. **Can the crawler read the page.** This is the gate everything else sits behind. OpenAI’s search
   crawler reads raw HTML only — if your content loads through JavaScript, it frequently sees a
   blank page. [A FAQ accordion that looks perfect to a
   visitor](/blog/why-faq-plugins-arent-a-good-idea) can be completely absent from the version a
   bot reads. No amount of waiting fixes this.
2. **Your robots.txt.** [Blocking the AI crawlers](/blog/is-my-site-blocking-chatgpt) sets your
   timeline to infinity. Worth noting that OpenAI’s systems take roughly a day to pick up changes
   after you edit `robots.txt`, so unblocking isn’t instant either.
3. **How often you already get crawled.** Sites that already appear in AI answers get crawled more
   often — it compounds once you’re in. A site with existing traffic and links gets picked up in
   days. A six-month-old site with three backlinks waits longer.
4. **Server speed.** AI crawlers are less patient than Googlebot. A slow page that Googlebot would
   retry may simply be skipped, or only partly fetched, by `PerplexityBot`.
5. **How the answer is written.** This is the difference between indexed and cited. Cited pages
   tend to lead with a direct answer in the first 50 words, and Q&A formatting is associated with
   roughly three times the citation rate. A page that buries the answer under three paragraphs of
   context gets passed over for one that doesn’t.
6. **Whether anyone is asking.** You can be perfectly crawlable and still never get cited, because
   nobody is asking a question your page answers. Citations only happen on real queries.

## How to make it faster

- **Publish as real HTML on your own domain.** Not a JavaScript widget. This is the single biggest
  lever, and it’s binary.
- **Allow the citation crawlers** — `OAI-SearchBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Claude-SearchBot` and
  `Google-Extended` — in your `robots.txt`.
- **Get into Bing.** Submitting your sitemap to Bing Webmaster Tools takes about ten minutes and is
  one of the highest-leverage things you can do, because ChatGPT Search leans on Bing’s index for
  live retrieval. IndexNow pushes new URLs to Bing instantly instead of waiting for a crawl cycle.
- **Put the answer in the first sentence.** Then the detail.
- **Be specific.** Prices, hours, service areas, timeframes. Vague marketing copy is unquotable.
- **Get mentioned elsewhere.** Being referenced on sites that are already indexed speeds up
  discovery of yours.

## What to expect at each mark

**Week 1.** Check your server logs. You should see AI crawlers hitting the new page. If you see
nothing at all, something is blocking them — that’s a technical problem, not a patience problem.

**Weeks 2–4.** Start asking the engines the questions a real customer would ask. You may see
nothing yet. That’s normal.

**Weeks 4–8.** First citations usually show up here, if they’re going to. Even with everything done
right, two to four weeks is a fair minimum before drawing conclusions — and if nothing has happened
well past that, the issue is usually relevance or authority rather than discovery.

**Day 90.** Now you have a real signal. If you’re cited for some questions and not others, you have
a gap list, which is exactly what you want.
