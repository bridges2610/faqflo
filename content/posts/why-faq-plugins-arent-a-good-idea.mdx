It looks great to your visitors. It’s invisible to the AI you’re trying to reach.

Installing a FAQ plugin feels like the obvious move. A few clicks, a slick little accordion on your
page, questions that expand when you tap them. Done. It looks professional, and your visitors can
find answers fast.

So here’s the uncomfortable part: the [AI answer engines](/blog/what-is-aeo) you actually want to
impress — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google’s AI Overviews — usually can’t see a word of it.

## The problem is JavaScript

Almost every FAQ plugin renders its answers with JavaScript. When a human opens your page, their
browser runs that JavaScript, the answers appear, and everything looks perfect.

AI crawlers don’t work that way. They generally grab the raw HTML of your page and move on — they
don’t sit around running JavaScript like a browser does. So when a FAQ plugin waits until after
page load to inject your answers, those answers simply aren’t in the HTML the crawler reads. Your
best content — the exact Q&A an AI would love to quote — is there for people and gone for machines.

You end up with a FAQ that looks great in a demo and gets you cited exactly zero times.

## “But it has SEO schema built in”

A lot of plugins advertise FAQ schema, and that used to earn you those expandable rich results in
Google. [Google retired FAQ rich results](/seo-guide) for most sites. So the headline reason many
plugins existed is gone — and the schema they inject client-side often can’t be read anyway, for
the same JavaScript reason.

Nice widget. Wrong job.

## What actually gets you cited

The fix isn’t fancy. Your answers need to live as real, crawlable HTML on your own domain — plain
text that’s in the page the moment it loads, no JavaScript required to reveal it. That’s the
version an answer engine can read, trust, and quote, with the citation pointing back to you.

A pretty accordion for your human visitors is fine on top of that. Just don’t let it be the only
place your answers exist, because that’s the version the AI never sees.
